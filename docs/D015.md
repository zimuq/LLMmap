## D — The question

> **Break the single `hard_subset` number (mean two-logit restricted
> accuracy over the 65 structural near-relative pairs, `METHOD.md §8`)
> into its 65 pair-level components, for `paper8`, `coverage`
> (`cvar_max`, γ=0.1), and `joint energy`, at matched `k`. Does the
> ≈0.4–0.5pp aggregate gain (D010, k=8) come from most pairs improving a
> little, a few pairs improving a lot while the rest are flat, or some
> pairs improving while others get worse (redistribution the aggregate
> would hide)?**

### What is and is not known going in

- The aggregate `hard_subset` numbers are already reported (D010, k=8:
  paper8 .9637, coverage .9644, joint energy .9685) and are small in
  absolute terms — the entire random-to-oracle range on this metric is
  only ~7.5 points (D008's own finding, the reason D008 was INCONCLUSIVE
  by the letter).
- Every run in D009/D010/D012/D013 already stores a `per_hard_pair` list
  of 65 floats per run (verified present, 2026-09-22). No new training,
  selection, or generation is needed — this is a read-and-reorganize job.
- The two previously-named difficult pairs (Falcon3-10B/7B,
  Phi-3-medium-128k/4k) are **not** derivation pairs — checked against
  `results/D001/model_metadata.csv`: both have an empty `base` field
  (no declared fine-tune parent/child relationship between the two
  models in the pair). They match on `lineage` only (`falcon-3`,
  `phi-3` respectively) — i.e. they are siblings released in the same
  model family/generation, not one derived from the other. Of the full
  65 structural pairs (37-model universe), 6 also share a literal `base`
  field (true fine-tune-of-shared-parent pairs), and those 6 are a
  subset of the 65 `same_lineage` pairs, not a disjoint category, in
  this dataset.
- What is *not* known: whether the aggregate gain is broad or narrow: no
  per-pair breakdown beyond the two named pairs has been reported.
- **Added 2026-09-22, per the human's question about where "hard" comes
  from for the two named pairs.** Their difficulty label has three
  independent sources, of different strength, none of them yet a
  trained/frozen/systematic protocol: (1) **D001** — an unsupervised
  open-set nearest-template AUC statistic computed on the *paper's own
  shipped artifact* (`default/` pretrained templates over 52 models),
  before this project generated any corpus of its own; Falcon3-7B↔10B
  was "the hardest pair in the entire matrix" there, Phi-3-medium-4k↔128k
  the 4th-hardest. External to our pipeline, but not a trained
  classifier. (2) **D008** — a single-query proxy *oracle* (best-of-pool,
  not a selected chain) on our own frozen `S_energy` tensor/corpus:
  0.840 / 0.800. Same corpus D009–D014 use, so not independent of it,
  and the review's 2026-09-22 audit (§4) already flagged this oracle
  number as a different estimand from a trained k-query classifier — not
  a ceiling on it. (3) **D010's saved per-seed trained numbers**
  (coverage .656–.800, joint energy .732–.832) — real trained accuracy,
  but never assembled into a clean per-pair report until this D. No
  source here is a prospectively-frozen, independent-development-set,
  trained-classifier measurement — see `H_ref` in Constraints.

## What counts as an answer

Report, per pair, per method, at `k`=8 (the project's standard headline
budget) and `k`=1 (smallest budget, for contrast):

- mean accuracy across the already-run 5 seeds, with a bootstrap CI
  computed the same way the project already computes per-cell CIs
  (paired at the config-bootstrap level, per D009's convention) —
  **note going in that these will be noisier than the aggregate**: each
  pair's number comes from ~50 evaluation traces (2 models × 25 test
  configs) × 5 seeds, an order of magnitude less data than the 65-pair
  average. State this caveat in `R`, don't let a narrow per-pair CI be
  read as a null result.
- Δ(joint energy − coverage) and Δ(coverage − paper8) per pair, flagged
  by sign — **explicitly list any pair where Δ is negative** (joint
  energy or coverage does worse than the comparator), not just the
  positive ones.
- Where the two previously-named pairs rank in this list — are they the
  hardest under every method (consistent with "structurally hard
  regardless of selection"), or does their rank change by method
  (consistent with selection-algorithm-dependent difficulty)?

**Not required:** a hardness ranking presented as an adopted definition
(`H_ref`) — see Constraints. **Not required:** worst-class per-pair
analysis, or any new `γ`/`k` combination beyond what D009/D010/D012/D013
already ran.

**Also required, for presentation use (added 2026-09-22):** the full
65-row table is the audit artifact, not the thing to hand to a
supervisor. Produce, alongside it, three short derived views (S6) —
a plottable γ×`k` curve for the two named pairs, a hardest-8 table, and
a per-family rollup — each under ~10 rows, each clearly labelled as a
*subset or aggregation of* the full table, not a separate measurement.

metadata**, plus F4's value-level corroboration, rather than claiming a stronger
check than the files support. All four sources have `per_hard_pair` on **100% of

**F1 — S3 cannot be built from the files it names, and D014 fixes it.** D012 and
D013 store runs only for the γ they *trained*; the γ they **reused under I6** have
no runs of their own. JointGreedy γ=0.1 lives in **D010**; GreedyCover γ=1.0 and

**Call 1 — include D014's cells in S3?** D014 closed 2026-09-19, *after* D015 was
drafted, and is not in its `deps` — but it holds `per_hard_pair` for **520 runs
across 13 more (algorithm, γ) cells** (GreedyCover γ ∈ {0.75, 0.6, 0.45, 0.4,
0.35, 0.3}; JointGreedy γ ∈ {0.75, 0.6, 0.5, 0.45, 0.4, 0.35, 0.3}). Including
them makes S3's curve **11 γ values per algorithm instead of 4–5, at zero
compute.** D015 says *"do not build a denser grid … nothing interpolated or newly
computed"* — I read that as a ban on **computing** new cells, which this is not,
but D014 is outside the stated deps so I am asking. **Proposal: include, labelled
by source D per cell as S3 already requires** — D014's own conclusion (γ is
continuous but strongly non-linear) is exactly what a γ-curve should show, and
showing it over 4 points when 11 exist would misrepresent the shape.

**F2 — use the per-pair config bootstrap, not a 5-seed one.** Every run stores
`cnt_pair` (65, 25): per pair, correct traces per test config. Proposal: one set
of 2,000 `boot_draws` multiplicity vectors shared across all methods and both `k`,
so every Δ is **paired on identical draws** — exactly the aggregate convention of
D009–D014, applied per pair. No new test (D015/S5).

**F3 — the resolution floor, and it should precede the tables in `R`.**

| | |
|---|---|
| effect being decomposed (joint − coverage, `k`=8) | **+0.41 pp** |
| `hard_subset`'s evidence base | 65 × 2 × 25 = **3,250 trace decisions** |
| what +0.41 pp is | **≈13 trace flips total** — 0.21 per pair |
| smallest step one pair can show | 1 trace of 50 = **2.00 pp** |

**The quantity being decomposed is an order of magnitude smaller than the
granularity of a single pair.** No pair *can* move by less than 2 pp, so "most
pairs improving a little" is arithmetically impossible to observe; whatever
appears will be a few pairs at ±2 pp and the rest at exactly zero — a pattern
forced by the denominator, not evidence about concentration.

**This sharpens the D rather than voiding it.** *"Does any pair get worse"* —
redistribution the aggregate hides — **is** fully answerable and is the half that
matters. *"Spread vs concentrated"* is **not answerable at 25 test configs**, and
`R` will say so rather than report the forced pattern as a finding. Same wall
D008/F3, D009, D010/R9 and D011 hit; it binds hardest here because the denominator
is per pair.

**F4 — a value-level corroboration, since metadata cannot supply one.** Report
Falcon3-10B↔7B's **rank among the 65 independently in each of the four sources**.
D001 (external, pre-corpus) and D008 (tensor oracle) both flag it hardest or
near-hardest; a permuted index would make its rank jump between files. Agreement
is corroboration, not proof, and `R` will label it so.
