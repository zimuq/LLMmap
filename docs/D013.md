## D — The question

> **Does CVaR-weighting (`γ<1`) produce a measurably better `GreedyCover`
> selection than mean-weighting (`γ=1`), once evaluated through D009's
> exact trained-classifier protocol — and does it show the same
> decisive, every-`k`-every-metric pattern D012 found for `JointGreedy`,
> or the narrower, worst-class-at-small-`k` pattern D008's own
> (untrained) proxy sweep suggested?**

D009 already trained exactly two points on this grid — `γ=1.0`
(`mean_greedy_max`) and `γ=0.1` (`cvar_max`) — as part of comparing
against `paper8`/`random`, not as a γ-ablation in its own right. D008's
full proxy sweep (`γ∈{1.0,0.5,0.25,0.1,0.05}`) was never trained beyond
those two points. This D trains the missing three.

### A structural prediction, pre-registered before running anything

**Hypothesis:** `GreedyCover`'s coverage is `MAX`-aggregated (I4), which
is monotone non-decreasing in `k` by construction (`METHOD.md §5.3`) —
*regardless of `γ`*. This is structurally different from `JointGreedy`'s
concatenated statistic, whose `γ=1.0` objective D012 found falls
monotonically past `k=1`. **Prediction: `GreedyCover`'s `γ=1.0`
selection objective will not show D012's pathology** (it should not
anti-correlate with trained accuracy) — so if `γ<1` helps `GreedyCover`
at all, it should show up as D008's own original proxy sweep suggested
(worst-class/worst-tail, concentrated at small `k`), **not** as the
uniform, every-`k`, every-metric dominance D012 found for `JointGreedy`.
**If the trained data instead shows `GreedyCover` behaving just as
decisively `γ`-dependent as `JointGreedy` did, this hypothesis is wrong
and `R` must say so plainly.**

**Update, 2026-09-18 — split verdict, from `## P` Part 0 (free, using
data that predates this D).** The *mechanism* half is confirmed:
`GreedyCover`'s objective is monotone in `k` at every γ, exactly as I4
predicts, and does not fall the way `JointGreedy`'s does — D012/R2's
pathology is `JointGreedy`-specific. **But the *prediction* half is
wrong**: D009's already-trained `γ=1.0`/`γ=0.1` endpoints show γ<1
beating γ=1 on mean top-1 at all 8 `k`, every CI excluding zero — the
D012-shaped pattern, not the narrow D008-shaped one predicted, and at a
comparable magnitude to `JointGreedy`. **This means D012/R2's own
explanation ("CVaR repairs an objective that fails outright") cannot be
the general reason γ matters** — `GreedyCover`'s objective has nothing
to repair and γ still helps just as much. Either a second, shared
mechanism exists, or D012/R2 over-attributed. Unresolved — S4's fuller
grid and S6's comparison are what this D still needs to run, but the
headline direction is already known from data that predates this D's
own drafting.

### Why this exists now

Cheap (D012 cost 9.4 minutes for a structurally identical grid), closes
`C1` symmetrically for both algorithms, and directly tests whether
D012/R2's mechanism (mean-aggregation degenerate past `k=1`) is a
`JointGreedy`-specific property of the concatenated statistic or a
general property of mean-aggregated selection. `LLMmap/greedy_cover.py`
and the trained-evaluation protocol both already exist — no new
infrastructure.

### R1 — The structural hypothesis: mechanism right, prediction wrong, as P1 reported before training

`GreedyCover`'s objective is **monotone in `k` at every γ** (γ=1.0: 1.3025 →
2.1050), exactly as I4's MAX-aggregation guarantees, against `JointGreedy`'s γ=1.0
falling 1.3025 → 0.8788. **D012/R2's pathology is `JointGreedy`-specific** — the
mechanism half holds.

The prediction half does not. The hypothesis inferred that a healthy objective
would make γ's benefit *narrow* (worst-class, small `k`). Measured: γ=0.1 and
γ=0.05 beat γ=1.0 on mean top-1 at **all 8 `k`**, with penalties (−0.044 to −0.110)
**comparable to `JointGreedy`'s** (−0.059 to −0.109), not narrower.

**The conclusion that outlives this D: objective monotonicity does not protect
against γ mattering.** `GreedyCover`'s objective rises healthily and it still loses
decisively, so D012/R2's reading — *"CVaR repairs an objective that fails
outright"* — **cannot be the general mechanism**; there is nothing broken here to
repair. Either a second, common mechanism is at work, or D012/R2 over-attributed.
D012's `## R` should carry an addendum saying so.

### R2 — D008/R6's γ=0.5 anomaly is **real, and it reproduces under training**

D008/R6 flagged γ=0.5 as non-monotone on the proxy and I called it *"what selection
noise looks like."* **I was wrong.** Trained:

- γ=0.5 is **statistically indistinguishable from γ=1.0**: 0/8 `k` exceed the seed
  range, and TOST returns **NULL-equivalence at `k`=5 and 6** — positive evidence of
  equivalence, not mere non-significance.
- It is **consistently worse than every other sub-1 value**, with F4's
  consistent-sign rule satisfied at all 8 `k` against each: **−0.072 vs γ=0.25,
  −0.094 vs γ=0.1, −0.095 vs γ=0.05.**
- On worst-class it is the **worst condition in the grid**, below even γ=1.0 at 6 of
  8 `k` (γ=0.5: 0.000–0.304; γ=1.0: 0.000–0.328).

**So γ's benefit is not monotone in γ — there is a threshold between 0.5 and 0.25.**
Reading γ as a continuous dial that trades mean against tail is wrong for
`GreedyCover`: 0.5 buys neither. The proxy saw this and I dismissed it; the trained
data confirms it. Mechanically, `CVaR₀.₅` averages the worse half of 666 pairs —
333 pairs, most of them not hard — so it neither tracks the mean (which uses all)
nor the tail (which needs the worst ~10%). It is the one setting that commits to
neither.

### Technical log

**What survives unqualified.** (i) `GreedyCover`'s objective is monotone
in k at every γ (I4) — the mechanism half of the hypothesis. (ii) γ ≤
0.25 beats γ=1.0 on mean top-1 at every k with the CI excluding zero for
γ ≤ 0.1 (8/8) — the effect-size prediction was wrong, as P1 reported
before training. (iii) The two algorithms' average γ-penalty is
indistinguishable, and validly so: the `hparams` hash, split, seeds and
bootstrap draws were asserted equal before any cross-algorithm number
was emitted.

**Wording on (iii).** "The same size" holds at the mean over k. Per-k
gaps differ by up to ~0.04 (e.g. k=3: −0.070 vs −0.109), above the 0.02
equivalence bound, so the defensible claim is "no detectable difference
in γ-sensitivity," not "equal." A writeup should not claim the
algorithms differ; it also should not claim they are proven the same.

**Why the γ=0.5 result is still worth having.** It is a real, trained
observation that a mid-range γ can fail to help — enough to retire "γ is
a clean continuous dial" as an assumption, and to make γ ≤ 0.25 a rule
rather than γ < 1. What it cannot yet support is a general threshold
claim; the check above would.

**TACC's self-correction (R7.3) is accepted and is the right lesson**:
D008/R6's "that looks like selection noise" was an unregistered
judgment, and "looks like noise" deserves the same pre-registration as
"looks like signal." The equally right caveat applies in the other
direction — one reproduced anomaly on one chain is a lead, not a law.

**Addendum, 2026-09-19 (from D014).** D014 tested the γ=0.5 question.
For `JointGreedy` a systematic, graded γ effect was found (chain luck
rejected). For `GreedyCover` — the algorithm this D concerns — the
chain-stability arm failed its positive control at `k`=4 and is
INCONCLUSIVE, so **this D's "no benefit at γ=0.5" remains a single-chain
observation, neither confirmed nor refuted.** D014 also shows the "same
γ-sensitivity" comparison above holds on the frozen full-data chains
only; under half-data resampling `GreedyCover`'s small-`k` γ effect is
unresolved. See `D014.md`'s Review.
