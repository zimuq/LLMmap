## D — The question

> **Does CVaR-weighting the joint statistic (`γ < 1`) produce a
> measurably better `JointGreedy` selection — on worst-class or
> worst-tail metrics, at any `k` — than mean-weighting it (`γ = 1`),
> once evaluated through D009's exact trained-classifier protocol? Or
> does the joint (concatenated) statistic already implicitly
> prioritize hard pairs well enough that `γ` stops mattering for this
> algorithm, unlike it did for `GreedyCover` (D008)?**

This is `C1`'s ablation, never run for `JointGreedy`. **Corrected
2026-09-16 (`TACC_NOTES.md` issue 14 — the original wording below
overstated D008's finding):** D008's γ-sweep for `GreedyCover` was
**never trained** — `results/D008/gamma_sweep.json` is the 1-NN proxy
over frozen embeddings; D009 trained only `cvar_max`, `mean_greedy_max`,
`paper8`, and `random`, none of which are γ-variants beyond those two
single points. And even at the proxy level, "helped worst-class" was
not a clearly resolved effect: D008's own bootstrap CIs at `k=8`
overlap substantially (γ=1.0 → 0.235 [0.120, 0.360] vs. γ=0.05 → 0.345
[0.200, 0.480]), γ=0.5 is non-monotone on mean top-1, and D008/R6
itself said the sweep *"does not separate"* 0.05 from 0.1. **So this D
is not just closing a `JointGreedy`-specific gap — it is the first
genuinely trained answer this project has to "does γ matter for query
selection at all,"** for either algorithm. Whether it matters for
`JointGreedy` is not assumed — it could go either way, and either
answer is useful.

### A mechanism worth pre-registering, not just running the grid blind

**Hypothesis (stated before any new run, per this project's standing
practice):** `GreedyCover`'s MAX-aggregated coverage needs `γ<1` because
under mean/`γ=1`, easy pairs (separable by many queries) can dominate
the objective and crowd out hard ones. `JointGreedy`'s advantage (D010)
comes from set-level *interaction* — and interaction plausibly matters
*most* exactly on hard, near-relative pairs, where no single query is
strong but combinations are (D010/R6's `q=0` finding). If that's right,
**mean-aggregation might already implicitly favor hard pairs for the
joint statistic**, in a way it never did for the per-query one — making
`γ` less necessary for `JointGreedy`, not equally necessary. **If the
grid shows `γ` mattering just as much for `JointGreedy` as it did for
`GreedyCover`, this hypothesis is wrong and `R` must say so plainly**,
not quietly drop it.

**Update, 2026-09-16 — refuted at the objective level, before any
training (`## P` Part 0).** TACC found this is measurable directly from
the selection objective: mean-aggregation's worst decile is only mildly
enriched for hard/near-relative pairs (15% vs. a 9.8% base rate) while
CVaR-weighting concentrates them far more (33%), and the worst-decile
CVaR score itself is 56% higher under `γ<1`. **Mean-aggregation does
not already capture the tail for the joint statistic — `γ` matters, in
the ordinary direction, and the hypothesis above is wrong.** Kept in
place as the record of what was predicted and why, per this project's
standing pre-registration discipline (D006/R11, D007/F2, D010/F1) — not
retroactively softened. What S5's training run actually tests is
different and still open: whether this real objective-level effect
survives to trained accuracy, especially since `JointGreedy`'s
mean-aggregated (`γ=1.0`) objective is itself sharply non-monotone in
`k` (peaks at `k=1`, declines every step after — `## P` F1), which
could produce a `γ=1.0` underperformance for a *different* reason
(dimension-dominated selection past `k=1`) than "CVaR helps."

### Why this exists now

Cheap, and it closes a genuinely open item (`C1`) for the algorithm the

`LLMmap/joint_greedy.py`) and D009 already established the

well-motivated axis of variation, `γ`, rather than opening new

## What counts as an answer

**Revised 2026-09-16, per `## P` F4 — the original wording made NULL
unreachable.** "Statistically indistinguishable" and "noisy" are not
opposites at this project's actual resolution (seed range 0.024–0.081
on mean top-1, worst-class CIs ±0.20) — every real result would satisfy
both, the same unsatisfiable-criterion class of error as D001's
censoring and D008's original CONFIRMED branch (TACC_NOTES issue 12).
Replaced with a pre-registered equivalence test (TOST): `δ = 0.02` on
mean top-1 — smaller than the smallest effect this project has ever
resolved (D010's +0.031 against a 0.038 seed range).

| Outcome | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **CONFIRMED (γ matters for JointGreedy)** | The 95% CI of a `γ`-to-`γ` difference excludes 0 **and** `\|Δ\|` exceeds the seed range (D009/F1's rule), on worst-class, worst-3, or mean top-1, at any `k`. The pre-registered hypothesis (mean-aggregation already captures the tail) is wrong — already contradicted at the objective level, per the update above; state whether it also holds at trained accuracy. |
| **NULL (γ doesn't matter for JointGreedy)** | The 95% CI of the `γ`-to-`γ` difference lies **entirely within ±δ** — positive evidence of equivalence, not merely a failure to detect a difference. **Not a failure** — it would mean the real, confirmed objective-level effect (`## P` Part 0) does not survive to trained accuracy. Recommend `γ=1` (mean) as `JointGreedy`'s default — simpler, no CVaR machinery needed. |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | Neither CONFIRMED nor NULL's condition is met — the data cannot distinguish "genuinely equivalent" from "not enough resolution to tell." Report which, per `γ`/`k`/metric cell, rather than forcing an aggregate call. |

it at the tensor level before any training — γ=1 leaves the worst decile at
CVaR₀.₁ = 0.1555 against 0.2434 for γ<1 (+56%), with hard pairs at 15% of that
decile against 33% — and the trained grid confirms it at every `k`.

Stating it as `## D` required: **the hypothesis is wrong.** γ matters *more* for
`JointGreedy` than D008's evidence ever showed it mattering for `GreedyCover`,
not less.

### R2 — But "CVaR helps" is not the whole mechanism, and F1's trace says why

P1/F1 flagged that γ=1.0's selection objective **peaks at `k`=1 and declines every
step after** (1.3025 → 0.8788), and pre-registered that if γ=1.0 underperformed,
the reading might be *"the mean joint objective is dimension-dominated"* rather
than *"CVaR helps"*. The trained numbers support the former as a large part of it:

- γ=1.0's **trained accuracy still rises** with `k` (0.540 → 0.803) while its own
  **objective falls monotonically**. So the objective it is maximising is
  anti-correlated with the outcome it produces, across the whole range.
- Its train/val gap at `k`=8 is **0.9394 / 0.8225**, against 0.992 / 0.892 for
  every sub-1 γ — γ=1.0 fits its training data *worse*, i.e. its selected queries
  carry less usable signal, not merely a worse-distributed one.

**So the honest statement is narrower than "CVaR-weighting helps":** under mean
aggregation the joint statistic is dominated by the dimensionality its own
concatenation adds, and past `k`=1 it stops being a sensible selection criterion
at all. CVaR-weighting is not improving a working objective — it is repairing one
that fails outright. Both readings predict "γ=1 loses"; the trace and the
train-accuracy gap distinguish them, and this D reports the distinction rather
than the flattering half.

### R3 — Within γ<1 there is no ordering, and `k`=8 is a three-way tie

F4's pre-registered equivalence test (δ = 0.02 on mean top-1), applied to all 24
γ-pair × `k` comparisons among the sub-1 values:

| comparison | NULL (equivalent) | different | INCONCLUSIVE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 vs 0.05 | 4 of 8 `k` | 2 | 2 |
| 0.25 vs 0.05 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| 0.25 vs 0.1 | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| **1.0 vs each** | **0** | **23 of 24** | 1 |

At **`k`=8 all three sub-1 values are NULL-equivalent to each other** (|Δ| ≤ 0.0006)
— expected, since P1/F2 found they select the identical set there. Below `k`=8 they
differ at individual `k` but **without a consistent ordering**: γ=0.1 leads at
`k`=1–3, γ=0.25 at `k`=5–6, γ=0.05 at `k`=7. That is what selection noise across a
nested chain looks like, not a γ-trend.

**Recommendation for C1: any γ ≤ 0.25 — γ = 0.1 as the default, on precedent
rather than evidence.** The data resolve *γ<1 vs γ=1* decisively and do **not**
resolve 0.25 vs 0.1 vs 0.05. Picking among them would be reading noise.

### R4 — F2's free finding: the trained network is insensitive to query order

At `k`=8, γ ∈ {0.25, 0.1, 0.05} select the **identical set**
`{0, 16, 114, 117, 140, 193, 233, 237}` in three different slot orders. Trained:

| | γ=0.25 | γ=0.1 | γ=0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean top-1 | 0.8763 | 0.8757 | 0.8763 |

**Across-order spread 0.0006, against a within-condition seed range of 0.0249 —
40× smaller.** So `build_traces`'s positional slot assignment does **not** make
the result order-dependent: the self-attention network treats the `k` slots as a
set, as its architecture implies but this project had never checked. Useful beyond
this D — it means any future chain can be reported as a set without an ordering
caveat.

### R5 — Cost, and the one thing that did not need running

**9.4 minutes** for 120 training runs; selection was 0.4 min (P1's S2). `hparams`
hash asserted identical to D009's on all 120. D010's γ=0.1 condition read from
`results/D010/`, never rerun (I6), with its chain asserted equal to
`[193, 140, 237, 114, 233, 117, 0, 16]` before anything else ran.

In hindsight the `k`=8 rows for γ=0.25 and γ=0.05 (10 runs) were predictable from
P1/F2 — the identical set had to give an identical answer up to seed noise, and it
did. They were worth running anyway: they are what measures R4, which was not
otherwise available.
