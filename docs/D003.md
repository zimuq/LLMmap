## D — The question

> **Can a proxy LLM, prompted per query-family with the paper's original 8
> queries and published baselines seeded in as anchors, produce a ~50–100-item
> candidate query pool suitable for Phase 1 — without hand-curation, and
> without the low-diversity failure mode where a bad pool makes every
> selection algorithm look equally mediocre?**

### Why this gates the mean-vs-CVaR comparison, not just Phase 1's convenience

The comparison that justifies CVaR-coverage over mean-greedy (`METHOD.md
§6.5`) needs a candidate pool large and diverse enough that the two
objectives can actually disagree on what to select. A pool that is small,
repetitive, or narrow in family coverage risks a **confound, not a null
result**: mean and CVaR could look indistinguishable not because the
objective doesn't matter, but because the pool never gave them anything to
disagree about. This D exists to produce that pool defensibly, and to leave
a record of how it was built so the confound can be ruled out later.

### Prior expectation, pre-registered

A single large generation pass tends to self-repeat in the back half of the
output, and a plain proxy LLM will not spontaneously reproduce the paper's
prompt-injection-wrapper structure (`))))]]]]}}}};*/...`) without being told
to. We expect **per-family, structured prompting** to be necessary, not
optional — this is a design constraint on the plan, not something P is free
to skip for convenience.

---

## What counts as an answer

| Outcome | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **READY** | Pool reaches target size (~100, or a smaller size explicitly justified against C3's eventual value), every one of the paper's four query families (`METHOD.md §5.4`'s generation target: meta-information, banner grabbing, malformed-alignment, weak-alignment) is represented including the prompt-injection-wrapper variant, and the anchor set (paper's 8 + `gpt4o-gen-opt` + `random-opt`, Table F.1) is present verbatim and tagged. |
| **NEEDS ANOTHER PASS** | Pool is short of target size or a family is thin/missing after the two-stage generation described below — re-prompt that family specifically rather than accepting a gap. |
| **BLOCKED** | The proxy LLM cannot produce a usable prompt-injection-wrapper variant even when explicitly instructed (e.g. refuses, or output is malformed in a way that breaks the query format) — report and escalate; do not silently drop that family. |

This is a construction task, not a statistical test — there is no
falsification criterion in the T0.1/T0.2 sense. "Answer" here means "pool
exists, is inspectable, and its provenance is auditable," not a verdict on a
hypothesis.

---

## Required steps (design points already settled — not open to reinterpretation by P)

**S1 — Generate by family, not in one pass.** Prompt the proxy LLM separately
for each of the four families, plus a separate explicit pass for the
prompt-injection-wrapper variant. Do not ask for the full ~100 in one
completion — the back half self-repeats.

**S2 — Seed with anchors, not from nothing.** Include, verbatim and tagged as
`anchor`:
- the paper's original 8 queries (`confs/queries/default.json`)
- the two published baseline sets — `gpt4o-gen-opt` and `random-opt`
  (Table F.1)

Two reasons this is required, not optional: (a) if mean-greedy's selection
ends up overlapping heavily with the original 8, that is a sanity check that
the separability tensor behaves reasonably; (b) it protects against the
failure mode where a low-diversity *generated* pool makes mean and CVaR
degrade together, which would look like "the algorithms are
indistinguishable" when the actual cause is "the pool is bad" — exactly the
confound this D exists to avoid.

**S3 — Tag every entry with its family and provenance** (`anchor` /
`generated-<family>`), in the same style as `METHOD.md §5.4`'s eventual
`Q_pool` bookkeeping — this pool is Phase 1's `Q_0`, and downstream code
will need the tags.

---

## Constraints

**Applicable invariants:** none of I1–I7 bind directly on generation itself.
I2 (split disjointness) and I3 (point-cloud comparison) apply once this pool
is *scored*, which is explicitly out of scope here (see below).

**Cost boundary — generation is cheap, scoring is not, and P must not blur
this.** The proxy-LLM step (producing ~100 query strings) is inexpensive and
is the entirety of this D's budget. **Scoring those candidates against the
full model universe is Phase 1's `|Q| × |L| × |S_build|`-scale cost**
(`METHOD.md §5.4`, already budgeted separately) and is explicitly **out of
scope for D003**. Do not let P quietly fold a scoring pass into this D's
cost estimate.

**Out of scope:** do not score candidates against any model, do not touch
the separability tensor, do not build or extend the corpus. This D produces
a candidate list and its provenance record — nothing else.

**Open question, deliberately left to P (not pre-decided here).** One
cost-reduction idea was raised in design discussion but **not decided**:
pre-filter the ~100 candidates on a small model subset before the full
Phase-1 scoring pass, trading completeness for cost — mirroring the
two-stage filtering already used in CDQD's outer loop generation step
(`METHOD.md §5.4` step D, stages 1–2). P may propose this or not; if
proposed, it must be disclosed as a distinct design choice with its own
tradeoff statement, not silently adopted. Whichever way P goes, flag it
explicitly in the plan for design-side review — this is exactly the kind of
routine-but-not-obvious call `CLAUDE.md`'s escalation rules ask to be
surfaced rather than decided invisibly.

### P1

**Status: PROPOSED — awaiting review.** Full plan: [`plans/D003-P1.md`](plans/D003-P1.md).

**Blocking prerequisite found: the anchors are not cleanly recoverable.** S2 requires
the two published baselines *verbatim*, but our only source is a text extraction of
the paper PDF, and Table F.1 is **two columns that extraction linearized into one
interleaved stream**, plus hyphenation breaks (`quan-\ntum`, `propri-\netary`). A
purely semantic split gives **7 gpt4o-gen-opt / 9 random-opt = 16**, while the paper
says the greedy procedure converged to **8** — so at least one entry is mis-assigned.
P1 §S0 tries the arXiv HTML rendering first and falls back to hand-reconstruction
with an 8/8 reconciliation and per-entry confidence flags. **If an 8/8 split cannot
be defended, the plan stops and reports** — these anchors exist to be a sanity check,
so a corrupted anchor set is worse than a missing one. (The paper's original 8 are
machine-readable in `confs/queries/default.json` — no ambiguity there.)

**Call 1 — the pre-filter question D003 left open: P1 proposes NOT to pre-filter.**
Grounds are methodological, not budgetary. A pre-filter ranks candidates by
discriminative power *averaged over the filter subset* — that is the **mean**
objective, so applying it before the mean-vs-CVaR comparison **bakes the mean
objective into the pool** and biases the experiment toward its own null: exactly the
confound this D exists to prevent. The paper also states that queries weak on their
own can be decisive in combination (§3.1), and a mean-based filter deletes precisely
those. And the saving is not needed — **D002 §R3 measured Phase 1 at 0.6% of the SU
balance** for 25×100 batched, with wall-clock rather than SUs binding. If design side
wants the pre-filter, run it as a separate tagged experiment *after* the unfiltered
comparison so its effect is measurable rather than baked in.

**Call 2 — generator (C6): recommend `Qwen/Qwen3-14B`, with the caveat stated.**
Perfect decoupling is unachievable — the 52-model universe spans ~20 organisations.
No Qwen3 is in the universe (only Qwen2/2.5) and it is ungated and capable, but it
**shares vendor lineage with universe members**, so Qwen-family self-preference
cannot be fully excluded. Strictly-decoupled alternative:
`allenai/OLMo-2-1124-13B-Instruct` (AllenAI has no presence in the universe) at some
cost in generation quality. Recommending Qwen3-14B because the anchors plus
per-family structured prompting constrain output far more than generator identity
does, and a weak generator risks the low-diversity failure mode this D guards
against. **Design side should overrule toward OLMo-2 if strict C6 compliance is
preferred over pool quality.** All six candidates verified available and ungated.

**Steps.** S0 recover/verify anchors (blocking) · S1 five separate generation passes
(four families + a dedicated prompt-injection-wrapper pass, per D003 S1) · S2 merge,
dedup against the frozen I5 embedding, tag with family/provenance — **anchors are
never deduped away** · S3 coverage check with targeted second passes for thin
families · S4 write R.

**Cost.** ~150 completions from one ≤14B model — well under one node-hour, one
`gh-dev` job. Scoring is explicitly excluded per this D's cost boundary.

**Docs slip (FYI, not corrected — `## D` is design-side-owned):** the "Notes for
whoever writes P" bind the generator to **C5**, but C5 in `DECISIONS.md` is the
outer-loop parameters; the generator-LLM decision is **C6**. Read as C6.

**Needs design side:** confirm/overrule the generator choice and the no-pre-filter
position before execution.
