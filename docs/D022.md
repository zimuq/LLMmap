## D — The question

> **Part A (primary, descriptive).** Under off-the-shelf **input-side**
> guard classifiers applied to the query text alone:
> - what fraction of the 259-query pool, and of each query family, is
>   flagged?
> - for each strategy (paper8, GreedyCover, JointGreedy, H1-attention,
>   H1-linear, random-from-pool) at `k`=1..8, how many of the first `k`
>   queries are flagged, and at which position is the chain first flagged?
>
> **Part B (ablation).** If JointGreedy (γ=0.1) selects only from the
> queries **no** primary guard flags, how much trained accuracy does it
> lose relative to the unrestricted JointGreedy chain, and does it still
> match paper8?

### Why this is worth doing, precisely

- **It is a risk check that is independent of every other open
  question.** It needs query text only: no responses, no generation, no
  new embeddings. It stays valid whatever D021's follow-up finds.
- **The comparison that matters is relative.** paper8 itself contains
  prompt-injection triggers (the paper's own +4% trick), so absolute
  flag rates will not be low for anyone. The question is whether our
  chains are materially *more* detectable than paper8, and what accuracy
  it costs to avoid that.
- **"First-flag position" is the unit an attacker cares about.** One
  blocked query can end a session. A chain flagged at position 1 is
  practically different from one flagged at position 7, even when their
  flag fractions are equal.

### Detectors (fixed here)

**Primary guards** (a query is "flagged" if any primary guard flags it at
its own default decision threshold; raw scores are stored too):

| id | role | default | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | prompt-injection / jailbreak detector | `protectai/deberta-v3-base-prompt-injection-v2` | small, openly licensed (P verifies the license) |
| **G2** | general input-safety classifier | IBM Granite Guardian (a 2B-class checkpoint; P names the exact one) | openly licensed (P verifies). Use its harm and jailbreak risk dimensions on the user turn |

**Optional guard:**

| id | role | default | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G3** | Meta Llama Prompt Guard 2 (86M) | include **only if** access is already available on TACC | Gated on the Hub. **Do not accept licence terms on anyone's behalf.** If access is missing, skip it and say so. It is optional, never blocking |

**Secondary: off-topic judge.**
- Setting: a query is sent to a *service* defined by a system prompt.
  Ask a judge LLM whether the query is on-topic for that service.
- Services: a fixed sample of 20 non-empty **S_build** system prompts,
  sampled with seed 20260928.
- Judge: must be outside both the 37-model universe and the D003 query
  generator (`OLMo-2-1124-13B-Instruct`), to avoid self-preference
  (`DECISIONS.md` C6's logic). P names it.
- Output: yes/no plus a short reason, greedy decoding, one fixed judge
  prompt that P writes out verbatim.
- Unit: the per-query "off-topic rate" across the 20 services.
- **If the judge's cost exceeds ~2 GPU-hours, P raises a Call instead of
  running it.**

### What is and is not known going in

- Our pool contains prompt-injection-wrapped variants and harmful-request
  (refusal) probes **by design**. They will be flagged. The family-level
  breakdown exists so a reader sees *which* families drive the rate, not
  only a total.
- Input-side guards only. Output-side filtering (the service's response
  being blocked or rewritten) needs generation and is **out of scope**.
- Whether a guard is actually deployed in front of a given service is
  unknowable. This D measures *would-be-flagged*, not *would-be-blocked
  in the wild*.

## What counts as an answer

**Part A.**
- (A1) Per-query table for all 259 queries: G1/G2/(G3) scores and flags,
  union flag, off-topic rate, family label.
- (A2) Flag rate by family, and overall.
- (A3) For each strategy and `k`=1..8: number flagged among the first
  `k`, and the first-flag position.
  - For random-from-pool, use the expectation over 1,000 draws (seed
    20260928), plus the distribution of the first-flag position.
- (A4) **Power vs stealth, descriptive.** Per-query Spearman between the
  union flag score (max over primary guards) and two per-query power
  measures:
  - CVaR₀.₁ over pairs of `S_energy_sf_tok200[q]` (what the selection
    algorithms see);
  - CVaR₀.₁ over pairs of D021's `M²_q`.
  - Report the sign and magnitude only; there is no decision rule.

**Part B.**
- **Restricted pool:** the queries not flagged by G1 or G2. G3 does not
  change the primary restricted pool; if it ran, report a G3-inclusive
  variant as sensitivity only.
- **Selection:** JointGreedy, γ=0.1, the same code path and settings as
  D010, restricted pool only, `k`=1..8.
- **Training:** the D009 attention protocol (5 seeds) and the D017/D020
  linear readouts (concat, mean-pool). Frozen hparams throughout.
- **Report, at each `k`:**
  - `JG-restricted − JG` and `JG-restricted − paper8`, mean top-1 with a
    paired config bootstrap.
  - Resolution rules: the linear readouts use CI-excludes-0 (D020's rule,
    no seed axis). The attention readout uses the project's standing
    D009/F1 rule.
  - The first-flag position of the restricted chain (by construction
    none under G1/G2; also report G3 and the off-topic rate if they ran).
- **No PARITY / WIN verdict is pre-registered.** Part B prices a
  trade-off; it does not test a hypothesis. Report the numbers with their
  intervals.

**Stop for a Call before Part B** if the restricted pool has fewer than
30 queries, or if any strategy's chain queries are missing from the
per-query table.

## Constraints

**Applicable invariants.**
- **I6:** every existing chain is reused verbatim. The single new chain
  comes from the same frozen tensor and corpus.
- **I2/I5:** Part B's training and evaluation use the standing
  build/val/test protocol and frozen embeddings. Detectors and the judge
  see query text and S_build system prompts only, never S_val/S_test
  responses.
- I1/I3/I4 are untouched.

**Exploratory label.** Part B's trained numbers use the same 25-config
S_test that D009–D021 inspected. Report them as exploratory, like every
comparison since D010 (09-22 review; design-side review M5).

**Out of scope:**
- output-side guards;
- generating responses under a guard;
- any ILP or constrained optimisation beyond the hard pool filter (the
  ILP follow-up's job);
- re-selecting GreedyCover or the H1 chains on the restricted pool;
- tuning detector thresholds.

**Cost:**
- detectors: minutes;
- judge: at most ~2 GPU-hours, or a Call;
- Part B: seconds of selection plus the usual ~10–15 min of training and
  fits.
- **State the measured cost in `R`.**
