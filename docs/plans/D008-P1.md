## Part 0 — Measured cost (D008 asks for a measured number, not an inherited one)

Measured on one `gh-dev` GH200 node (job 977811), at the real shapes:

| component | measured | full-scale cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| load build cubes (37 × 259 × 75 × 1024) | 3.6 s | — |
| load test cubes (37 × 259 × 25 × 1024) | 1.2 s | — |

**Total ≈ 1.5 core-hours, no GPU, one `gh-dev` job well inside the 2 h cap.**

The single number that shapes the whole plan is the 0.02 s greedy chain. Once
the 259 per-query distance matrices exist (197 s, once), *every* condition, every
`k`, every `γ`, and every random seed is table arithmetic — see F0 below. That is
why this plan can afford 200 random seeds, a full 5-point `γ` sweep, a full MMD
tensor for S6 rather than a subset, and a selection-stability check (F1), instead
of subsampling any of them.

**Disclosure — what the benchmark already revealed.** It ran `GreedyCover` twice
on the frozen tensor to time it. At `k=8`, `γ=0.1` and `γ=1.0` select
**overlapping in only 2 of 8 queries**. No accuracy was computed. This rules out
the most trivial mechanism for a FALSIFIED verdict ("the objective doesn't matter
because both objectives pick the same queries") — it does **not** indicate the
direction of the result, since different sets can still classify identically.

## F0 — The design decision that makes everything else cheap (not a Call, just how it is built)

Squared Euclidean distance on a concatenated `k`-query trace **decomposes
additively over the queries**:

```
d²(trace_s, ref_t) over Q_k  =  Σ_{q ∈ Q_k}  ‖E_q(s) − E_q(t)‖²
```

So the per-query eval×ref matrices `D_q` (925 × 2775) are computed once, and a
`k`-query set's full distance matrix is the **sum of `k` of them**. Nearest
neighbour by Euclidean distance is nearest neighbour by squared Euclidean
distance, so nothing is lost. This is why the 200-seed random baseline and the
`γ` sweep cost nothing, and it is the reason Call 1's proposal below is a
nearest-neighbour classifier rather than a trained one.

## Call 1 (design side) — turning a selected `k`-query set into an accuracy number

**Proposal.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **trace** | one (model `v`, config `s`) pair, represented by the I5 embeddings of its responses to the `k` selected queries, concatenated (`k × 1024`) |
| **reference set** | the **build** split — 37 models × 75 configs = 2,775 reference traces |
| **evaluation set** | the **test** split — 37 models × 25 configs = **925** eval traces |
| **classifier** | 1-nearest-neighbour, Euclidean, 37-way (chance = 2.7%) |
| **secondary check** | nearest-centroid and a 37-class logistic probe, **on `val` only**, at `k ∈ {1, 4, 8}` |

**Why this and not D004's M2 verbatim.** D004 used *leave-one-out* NN because it
had exactly one pool of traces and no splits. We have I2-disjoint splits, so LOO's
optimism is unnecessary: build is the reference, test is held out, and the config
parameters (system prompts, temperatures, RAG/CoT templates) do not cross between
them. It is D004's M2 with the leave-one-out replaced by the split it was
standing in for — the same estimator, measured honestly.

**Why NN rather than something trained.** Three reasons, in order of weight:
(1) it is what D004 established as this project's classification instrument;
(2) it is what the shipped LLMmap model natively exposes (Euclidean distance to
per-model references, `LLMmap/inference.py:178`); (3) it decomposes per F0, which
is what makes the 200-seed band and the `γ` sweep affordable. A trained classifier
would have to be refit for every (condition, `k`, seed) — thousands of fits — and
would introduce its own capacity/regularization confound into a comparison whose
whole point is to isolate the *selection objective*.

**Leakage statement.** Selection reads only the build-split tensor. The classifier
references only build. Test is touched once, to report. `val` is used only for the
secondary classifier-form check (and F4's), never for selection or thresholds.

**Metrics (all four, `METHOD.md §8`), at every `k`:**
- **mean top-1** over the 925 eval traces;
- **worst-class top-1** = min over the 37 models of per-model accuracy (25 traces
  each) — plus the **mean of the worst 3 classes**, for the reason in F3;
- **hard-subset accuracy** — Call 2;
- **queries needed to reach X%**, X ∈ {50, 70, 80, 90} for both mean and
  worst-class; smallest `k` on the condition's own chain, or `">k_max"`.

**Uncertainty.** Paired bootstrap over the **25 test configs** (resample configs
with replacement, 2,000 draws), not over traces — the 37 traces sharing a config
are not independent. Report the 95% CI of each condition's difference from
mean-greedy at each `k`. The random baseline additionally gets a 5th–95th
percentile band over its 200 seeds.

## Call 2 (design side) — what "hard subset" means in our 37-model universe

**Proposal: the 65 structural near-relative pairs, at the pair level.**

Defined from `results/D001/model_metadata.csv` as same `base` **or** same
`lineage` — the identical rule D004 and D007's S2a already used. Among the 37
models this yields **65 pairs of 666 (9.8%)**, spanning 33 models, headlined by
`Falcon3-10B ↔ Falcon3-7B`, `Phi-3-medium-128k ↔ Phi-3-medium-4k`,
`Meta-Llama-3-8B ↔ 3.1-8B`.

**Hard-subset accuracy** = mean over those 65 pairs of the **2-way restricted
top-1 accuracy**: restrict the reference set to the two models, classify their 50
eval traces. Also report the **minimum** over the 65 (the worst hard pair) and
`Falcon3-10B ↔ Falcon3-7B` by name, for continuity with D004/D007.

**Three reasons for pair-level and structural:**

1. **Structural is not circular.** The obvious alternative — "the empirically
   hardest pairs" — would be defined *from the same tensor the selection
   optimizes*, so a CVaR objective that targets the tensor's tail would be scored
   on the tail it targeted. The near-relative labels come from model provenance
   and were fixed in D001, before any of this existed.
2. **Model-level does not work here.** 33 of the 37 models appear in at least one
   near-relative pair, so "37-way accuracy restricted to hard models" is 89% of
   the eval set — it would be mean accuracy with extra steps.
3. **The paper's literal hard subset is out of universe.** Llama-3-70B ↔
   Smaug-70B was excluded by A1 (≤14B). D008 anticipates exactly this.

**Reported alongside, not instead:** the empirical bottom decile (the 67 pairs
with lowest pool coverage `max_{q ∈ Q_0} S_energy[q][p]`) and its **overlap with
the 65 structural pairs**. That overlap is itself worth having — it is the direct
test of I1's premise that hardness tracks structure, and D007/R4 saw it hold for
the top 3 pairs unprompted.

## Call 3 (design side) — `θ` for the identifiability frontier

A single percentile threshold is the weakest available option: it fixes the size
of the "frontier" by construction, so the deliverable can never come back empty
and never carries information about whether the frontier exists at all.

**Proposal: report the frontier at three tiers, and say plainly which one is
which.**

| tier | `θ` | claim it supports |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 — statistically indistinguishable** | 95th percentile of the **self-pair null** (same model, its 75 build configs split in half, same query) | "no query in the pool separates this pair beyond what the same model produces against itself" |
| **T2 — operationally unidentifiable** *(primary deliverable)* | best-query **2-way test accuracy < 75%** (chance 50%), where the query is selected on the build tensor and evaluated on test | "no query in the pool lets us tell these two apart in practice" |
| **T3 — relative** | 5th percentile of pool coverage (33 pairs) | comparability with a percentile-style reading; explicitly labelled as definitionally non-empty |

**Expected outcome of T1, stated in advance so it is not spun afterwards.** The
null value of scale-free energy at cell size *n* is ≈ `2/n` (the `cdist(A,A)`
diagonal makes the estimator upward-biased by one mean-distance per sample), i.e.
≈ 0.03–0.05 here. The observed pool-coverage **minimum is 0.289** and the 1st
percentile is 0.63 — an order of magnitude above. **T1's frontier will almost
certainly be empty**, and that is a real result: *every* pair in this universe is
statistically separable by some query in `Q_0`, which is why T2 (operational) is
proposed as the primary. T1 is cheap (9,583 null cells ≈ 1 core-minute) and is
what lets us say "empty" rather than "we chose a threshold that made it empty".

Note the null is measured at n=37/38, not 75; the n-dependence is reported at
n ∈ {25, 37} and the **conservative (largest) value** used, since 150 same-model
configs per (query, model) do not exist (only 125 do).

For every frontier pair at every tier: same base? same lineage? adjacent version?
same architecture? same tokenizer? — the D004 diagnostic block, as `METHOD.md §7`
requires this to be a standalone deliverable.

## F1 — Proposed addition: is the *selected set* stable enough for a k=1–3 comparison to mean anything?

D008's CONFIRMED criterion includes "the small-`k` portion of the curve (k=1–3)
rises". At `k=1` the entire verdict rests on **which single query** greedy picks
out of 259 — and D007/R8 recorded that the tensor's cells are noisy (75 vs 75 in
1024-d). If the pick is unstable across resamples of the build configs, a
difference at `k=1` is estimator noise, not objective.

**Proposal (cost ≈ 0.5 core-h):** split the build split's 75 configs into two
disjoint halves, recompute `S_energy` on each (2 × 0.26 core-h), run both
objectives on each half, and report (a) the overlap of the two `k=8` chains,
(b) the test accuracy of each half's chain. Same split-half logic as D007's F2,
applied to the selection instead of the aggregation.

**This is a precondition for interpreting S3, not an extra.** If half-A's and
half-B's chains disagree substantially *and* their accuracies differ, then the
CVaR-vs-mean gap must be read against that variability, and P recommends saying so
in `R` rather than quoting a point difference.

## F2 — S6's MMD needs a **global** bandwidth, or it repeats D007/F3's mistake

The textbook RBF-MMD uses a median-heuristic bandwidth computed **per cell**. That
makes the statistic's scale cell-dependent — and I4 aggregates with a MAX
**across queries**, exactly the operation D007/F3 established is invalid for a
statistic whose scale varies by query.

**Proposal:** one global bandwidth `σ²` = median pairwise squared distance over a
large random sample of cells, fixed once and used for all 172,494 cells. The
per-cell median-heuristic variant is computed too, and reported only as a
diagnostic — the same raw/scale-free split D007 settled on.

## F3 — Worst-class accuracy has 4-percentage-point resolution, and D008's verdict leans on it

25 test configs per model means per-model accuracy moves in steps of 1/25 = 4 pp.
The CVaR objective's *headline* metric is therefore the coarsest thing measured
here, and a 1-model, 1-trace flip moves it a full step.

**Mitigations, all reported:** (a) the **mean of the worst 3 classes** alongside
the strict minimum — smoother, and it is what CVaR actually optimizes anyway;
(b) bootstrap CIs over configs; (c) a **replication on the `val` split** (25 more
configs) — with the caveat that D006/S3's Call 3 gave `val` and `test` *shared*
CoT and RAG prompt pools (they are disjoint from build, not from each other), so
this is a near-replication, not an independent one.

**If the CVaR-vs-mean gap at every `k` is smaller than 4 pp on worst-class
accuracy, P recommends INCONCLUSIVE on that metric specifically**, not a verdict
read off the point estimates. Pre-registering that now.

## F4 — The concatenated-Euclidean classifier silently weights queries by embedding norm

This is the one that would corrupt the comparison quietly. Under F0's summed
distance, a query whose responses have larger embedding norms contributes more to
every trace distance than a query whose responses are short. So the classifier
applies an implicit, unexamined weighting across the `k` selected queries — while
selection under I4 treats them symmetrically via MAX. **This is D007/F3's finding
(raw energy is not comparable across queries under unnormalised embeddings)
reappearing on the classifier side of the same pipeline.**

**Proposal:** primary = **per-query scale-normalised** distances, `D_q` divided by
the mean within-reference distance for query `q` — the exact analogue of the
scale-free convention the tensor already uses, and consistent with what selection
optimizes. Raw concatenation is computed and reported as sensitivity. **Which one
headlines is decided on `val`, before test is touched**, and both are reported
regardless.

Note this changes nothing about F0: the normalisation is a per-query scalar, so
the sum decomposition still holds and the cost is unchanged.

## Minor decisions (taken, not escalated — flagged so they are visible)

- **The paper's 8 queries are `Q_0[0..7]` exactly** — verified against
  `confs/queries/default.json`. Condition 1 is therefore directly evaluable on the
  frozen tensor with no re-generation.
- **Paper-8 at `k < 8`:** the file's order is arbitrary, so a prefix would be an
  artifact. Report the **mean over all `C(8,k)` subsets** (≤70 per `k`, free).
- **Random-`k`:** 200 independent uniform draws per `k`, not a nested chain
  (a nested random chain is a different, easier baseline). 200 gives a stable
  5–95% band; the cost is nil.
- **`k_max = 16` for the "queries to reach X%" metric only.** With 37 classes,
  some conditions may not reach 90% within `k ≤ 8`, and ">8" for every condition
  answers nothing. The headline curve stays `k = 1..8` per D008.
- **Tie-breaking in greedy:** lowest query index, deterministic; ties are counted
  and reported (with continuous energy values they should not occur).
- **CVaR convention:** `mean of the lowest ⌊γ·|pairs|⌋ values`, `k = max(1, ⌊γ·n⌋)`
  — byte-identical to D004's `cvar_low` and D007's `cvar`, so the numbers are
  comparable across D's.

**S1 — `LLMmap/greedy_cover.py`.** `GreedyCover(S, k, gamma, agg)` returning the

(a) `CVaR(x, 1.0) == mean(x)` exactly; (b) the `γ=1.0` chain equals the chain from

— D008 requires this reduction verified numerically; (c) coverage is monotone
non-decreasing in `k`; (d) selected queries are distinct.

**S2 — Coverage rule with `γ` and `agg ∈ {max, sum}`.** MAX is I4 and the default;
`sum` exists only as the isolation variant D008's S2 asks for, and is used for

comparison isolates the selected set.

**S3 — The five conditions**, all on the identical frozen tensor: paper-8;
random-`k` (200 seeds); mean-greedy (`γ=1.0`, MAX); CVaR-coverage (`γ=0.1`, MAX);
mean-greedy with `sum`. Emit the selected query text for each condition — a reader
should be able to see what a CVaR objective actually picked.

**S4 — Metrics** per Call 1 and Call 2, four metrics × 5 conditions × `k=1..8`
(+`k≤16` for queries-to-X), with bootstrap CIs → `metrics_by_condition_k.json`.
**Waits on Call 1 and Call 2 dispositions.**

**S5 — `γ` sweep** over {1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05}, MAX, mean-vs-worst-class
trade-off curve → `gamma_sweep.json`. Also report **chain overlap between adjacent
`γ` values** — if the selected sets are identical the trade-off curve is flat by
construction, and that mechanism should be reported rather than inferred.

**S6 — Statistic sensitivity.** Full MMD tensor (global bandwidth per F2; 0.51
core-h, so no subsetting), then: per-cell Spearman vs `S_energy`; overlap of the
`k=8` chains under both statistics at `γ ∈ {0.1, 1.0}`; and whether the
**accuracy ranking of the five conditions** changes when selection runs on MMD.
Per D008: if the sets or the ranking move materially, S3–S5 are reported as
partly statistic-dependent, in `R`, not quietly.

**S6b — Selection stability** per F1 (split-half of the build configs).

**S7 — Identifiability frontier** per Call 3, three tiers, with the D004
structural diagnostics → `identifiability_frontier.json`. **Waits on Call 3.**

**S8 — Write `## R`:** verdict against D008's outcome table, all four metrics at
every `k` for all five conditions, the `γ` curve, S6's robustness result, S6b's

dispositions and F1–F4 outcomes. Mean-greedy is described as *"our reconstruction

## Failure handling

- **Any frozen input fails S0's assertions** — stop, report. Do not rebuild
  anything; I6 forbids it and D007's tensor is the reference.
- **The `γ=1.0` ≡ mean-greedy reduction does not hold numerically** — stop. That
  is a bug in `GreedyCover`, and every S3 number would be meaningless. Report
  rather than tune the tolerance.
- **CVaR ≈ mean-greedy on all metrics** — that is D008's pre-registered FALSIFIED
  branch, not a failure. Report it as the result, note that the two objectives
  nonetheless selected largely different query sets (Part 0), and stop there:
  Phase 3 is explicitly out of scope for this D.
- **Gap smaller than the worst-class metric's 4 pp resolution** — INCONCLUSIVE on
  that metric, per F3, stated as such.
- **S6's MMD chains disagree materially with `S_energy`'s** — report as a
  statistic-dependence limitation on S3–S5, per D008's own falsification wording.
- **Cost guardrail:** stop and report past 6 core-hours (4× the measurement).
- **`S_probe` is not a fallback anywhere in this D** (D007/R1, and D008 says so
  twice). If any step seems to want a bounded statistic, report it instead.

## Verification

- `GreedyCover` chains are nested: `Q^(k) ⊂ Q^(k+1)` for every condition and `γ`.
- Coverage under MAX is monotone non-decreasing in `k`; CVaR of coverage likewise.
- Mean top-1 at `k=1` for the *best single query* must exceed chance (2.7%) by a
  wide margin, and random-`k`'s band must contain the mean-greedy point at `k=259`
  (degenerate check: with all queries selected every condition coincides).
- `D_q` sanity: the diagonal blocks (a model against itself) must have the
  smallest mean distance for the large majority of queries.
- Re-running from the frozen tensor + corpus reproduces every JSON bit-for-bit
  (all seeds fixed and recorded).
- Sum checks: 666 = C(37,2); 65 structural pairs; 925 = 37 × 25 eval traces;
  2,775 = 37 × 75 reference traces.
