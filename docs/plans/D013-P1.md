## Part 0 — The structural hypothesis: mechanism right, prediction wrong, both already decidable

### The mechanism half is **confirmed**, for free

| γ | `GreedyCover` objective, `k`=1→8 | monotone? |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1.3025 → 1.5522 → … → **2.1050** | ✅ |
| 0.5 | 0.4684 → … → 1.1641 | ✅ |
| 0.25 | 0.2740 → … → 0.8237 | ✅ |
| 0.1 | 0.1788 → … → 0.6194 | ✅ |
| 0.05 | 0.1456 → … → 0.5226 | ✅ |

Monotone at **every** γ, exactly as I4's MAX-aggregation guarantees. Against
D012's `JointGreedy` at γ=1.0: **1.3025 → 0.8788, falling every step.**

**A detail worth keeping:** both algorithms start at **exactly 1.3025** at `k`=1 and
diverge from there. That is not a coincidence — at `k`=1 the joint statistic reduces
to the per-query one (D010/R2 verified this to the bit), so the two algorithms *are*
the same algorithm at `k`=1. It is a free consistency check across three D's, and
it confirms the divergence is caused purely by how each aggregates as `k` grows.

**So D012/R2's pathology is `JointGreedy`-specific.** That part of the hypothesis
is right, and S6's question is answered before training starts.

### The prediction half is **already contradicted by D009's own trained data**

The hypothesis infers from monotonicity that γ's benefit for `GreedyCover` should
be *narrow* (worst-class, small `k`) rather than D012's uniform dominance. **D009
already trained both endpoints**, and they say otherwise:

**Mean top-1, positive at all 8 `k`, every CI excluding zero, 6 of 8 exceeding the

*"CI excludes 0 and |Δ| exceeds seed range, on mean top-1, at most or all `k`"* —
not the D008-shaped branch the hypothesis predicted.

And the magnitudes are comparable between algorithms, not narrower for
`GreedyCover`:

| `k` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `JointGreedy` γ=1 penalty | −0.093 | −0.101 | −0.109 | −0.080 | −0.068 | −0.062 | −0.059 | −0.073 |

### Why this matters beyond D013

**Objective monotonicity does not protect against γ mattering.** `GreedyCover`'s
γ=1.0 objective rises healthily and it *still* loses decisively. So D012/R2's
narrow reading — *"CVaR is repairing an objective that fails outright"* — **cannot
be the general explanation for why γ helps**, because here there is nothing broken
to repair and γ helps just as much. Either there is a second, common mechanism, or
D012/R2 over-attributed. **This sharpens D012/R2 rather than contradicting it**, and
it is the most interesting thing D013 produces. It is also, notably, available from
data that existed before this D was drafted.

## F1 — D013's verdict is substantially pre-determined, and P1 says so rather than discovering it in `R`

The CONFIRMED-shape question is settled by D009's two trained points (Part 0). The
three new γ values cannot un-resolve eight CIs that already exclude zero. **What
S4 actually buys, stated honestly:**

1. **D008/R6's γ=0.5 anomaly** — the proxy had γ=0.5 *below* γ=1.0 on mean top-1 at
   `k`=3–4 (0.636 vs 0.697), which I flagged as looking like selection noise. Its
   chain is the most distinctive of the five (`[117, 233, 234, …]`, sharing only
   `117` with γ=1.0's first pick), so this is a real test, not a formality.
2. **Whether γ's benefit is monotone in γ** for `GreedyCover` — D012 found no
   ordering among sub-1 values for `JointGreedy`; whether that repeats here is open.
3. **S6's cross-algorithm comparison at every γ**, rather than at the two endpoints
   D009 happens to have trained.

None of that changes the verdict; all of it changes what the `R` table can say.
**Recommendation: run it — 120 runs at ~9 minutes is not a cost worth optimising
against — but the `## R` verdict line should cite D009's data as its primary
evidence, with the new points as the curve around it.**

## F2 — S6's comparison is valid, but only because of a detail worth stating

D013/S6 says the cross-algorithm comparison is *"finally valid (unlike D012/S6)"*.
Correct, and the reason is narrower than "both are now trained": they are trained
**on the same `hparams` hash, the same I2 split, the same 5 seeds, and the same
bootstrap draws**, so a `GreedyCover`-vs-`JointGreedy` delta at fixed γ and `k` is
*paired*. P1 will assert the hash equality across all three D's (D009's
`0ce04efbd56c…`) before emitting any cross-algorithm number, and record it in the
comparison artifact — otherwise "valid" rests on assumption rather than check.

## F3 — At `k`=1 the two algorithms are identical, so their `k`=1 rows are not independent evidence

Both objectives are the mean/CVaR over pairs of the same single-query statistic at
`k`=1, both select the same query at each γ (γ=1.0 → `q=123`, γ=0.1 → `q=193` for
*both* algorithms), and both measured **−0.0929**. S6 must not report that as two
agreeing observations — it is one observation appearing twice. Reported as a
**consistency check** instead, which is what it is, and a good one.

## F4 — Reuse D012's TOST as approved, with one carry-over caveat

δ = 0.02 on mean top-1, as D013 specifies. One thing to carry forward: D012/R3 found
that below `k`=8 the sub-1 γ values differ at individual `k` **without a consistent
ordering**, and concluded that picking among them would be reading noise. The same
risk applies here, and `R` will apply the same discipline: a γ-to-γ difference among
sub-1 values gets reported as an ordering **only** if its sign is consistent across
`k`, not from individual significant cells.

## Failure handling

- **γ=0.5 reproduces the proxy's anomaly under training** (below γ=1.0 at some `k`):
  report it as a real property of that chain, not noise — the proxy and trained
  levels agreeing on an oddity is stronger evidence than either alone.
- **A γ-to-γ ordering appears among sub-1 values**: apply F4 — report as an ordering
  only on consistent sign across `k`.
- **Any cross-algorithm hash mismatch**: stop and report. S6's validity is the whole
  point of the step.
- **Cost guardrail:** stop past 1 GPU-hour (6× the projection).
