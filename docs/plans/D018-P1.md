## S0 — all five arms priced

2,044 candidate evaluations per arm (259+258+…+252). Per-evaluation cost measured
at `k` = 1, 4, 8 (3 candidates each, median), then weighted by the real candidate
count at each greedy step:

| arm | `k`=1 | `k`=4 | `k`=8 | **projected full search** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **attention network** | 3.1 s | 5.4 s | 3.8 s | **2.47 h** |
| linear concat, tight (`20000/1e-6`) | 3.2 s | 9.2 s | 18.5 s | **5.98 h** |
| linear concat, loose (`3000/1e-4`) | 2.4 s | 4.9 s | 7.0 s | 2.79 h |
| linear mean-pool, tight | 4.5 s | 3.8 s | 4.0 s | 2.30 h |
| **linear mean-pool, loose** | 2.9 s | 2.3 s | 2.3 s | **1.39 h** |

**Three things worth stating because they contradict expectations in the D:**

1. **The attention network is not the expensive arm.** `PLAN.md` projected ~5 h
   from D009's 8.7 s/run; measured here it is **3.1–5.4 s/eval → 2.47 h**. D009's
   8.7 s included checkpointing and full test evaluation; a search step needs
   neither.
2. **`k`-scaling runs in opposite directions.** The attention network is
   essentially `k`-flat (3.1 → 5.4 → 3.8 s, non-monotone — early stopping fires at
   different epochs). Linear concat grows **6×** from `k`=1 to `k`=8 (3.2 → 18.5 s),
   because features = `k`×2048. **That is why concat-tight is the single most
   expensive option at 5.98 h** — the search spends most of its evaluations at
   large `k`, exactly where concat is worst.
3. **Solver tolerance is worth 2.1× on concat** (5.98 → 2.79 h) and 1.7× on
   mean-pool.

## Call 1 — greedy-step selection on `S_val`, as D018 proposes. Confirmed.

Each step's `argmax A` is evaluated on **`S_val`**; `S_test` is touched exactly once
per chain per `k`, at S2. This follows `PAPER_DEVIATIONS.md` items 6 and 13 and the
human's standing instruction that reproducing the pipeline does not mean reproducing
its unreasonable choices (`METHOD.md §6.2`: Algorithm H.1 "selects on `T_test` and
reports on the same distribution").

**One consequence I want on the record before the result exists:** this makes the
comparison *harder* for H.1, not easier. Its own protocol would let it select on the
split it is scored on — an advantage worth some accuracy. **So a PARITY-OR-BETTER
outcome is not "we beat H.1 as specified"; it is "we beat H.1 run under a fairer
protocol than it specifies."** That distinction belongs in `R` and in any writeup
sentence built on it. If design side wants the literal circular variant as a
sensitivity arm it is +2.5 h for the attention side, and I would rather be asked
than assume.

## Call 2 — one seed during the search, 5 for the final chain. Confirmed as proposed.

D018's own argument is right and I have nothing to add: Algorithm H.1's "372
training runs" implies one run per candidate, so a single seed during the search is
*more* faithful, not a compromise. Five seeds would make the attention arm 12.4 h.
The final `k`=1..8 chains get this project's standard protocol (5 seeds for
attention, deterministic fit + config bootstrap for linear), so the reported
comparison is on the same footing as every prior D.

**Stated risk:** a single-seed argmax can pick a query that won on seed noise. At
`k`=8 the attention network's seed range is 0.024–0.081 on mean top-1 (D009/F1),
and candidate-to-candidate differences at a greedy step will often be smaller than
that. **So the chain itself is noisy — that is a property of Algorithm H.1 as
specified, not of this implementation**, and `R` will say so rather than presenting
the chain as canonical. D014's Arm B measured exactly this kind of selection
instability for the tensor-based criteria; the same caveat applies here with more
force, since there is no averaging at all.

## Call 3 — **mean-pool for the linear search, both poolings for the final chain.**

D018 asks for this decision *after* S0, and S0 makes it:

- **Concat-tight (5.98 h) is not worth 4.6× mean-pool-loose (1.39 h)** for a step
  whose only output is an argmax over candidates.
- **Loosening the solver for the search is the wrong lever.** D017/Call 1 showed
  the loose setting produces a 0.004 order-dependent wobble from non-convergence.
  At a greedy step, differences between candidates are often that size, so a
  loose-solver argmax could be picking convergence noise. **Mean-pool at the tight
  setting (2.30 h) avoids both problems** — `k`-flat cost *and* converged fits.
- **Proposal: run the linear search with mean-pool at `max_iter=20000, tol=1e-6`
  (2.30 h), and evaluate the resulting chain at S2 under *both* poolings**, so the
  final number is comparable with D017's concat-primary headline.

**Disclosure of what this costs in fidelity:** D017/R4 measured concat − mean-pool
at **+3.7 to +7.0 pp**, so the search is being guided by a readout weaker than the
one the final numbers are reported under. That is a real asymmetry. It is the same
asymmetry Algorithm H.1 itself has (it searches with whatever classifier it has),
and `R` will state it rather than leave it implicit.

**Total proposed: attention 2.47 h + linear mean-pool tight 2.30 h ≈ 4.8 GPU-hours**,
plus ~15 min for S2's final evaluation. Within the accepted envelope.

## F1 — The two arms answer different questions, and only one of them is the paper's

The attention arm is Algorithm H.1 with the paper's own classifier. **The linear arm
is not Algorithm H.1** — it is the same search procedure with a classifier the paper
never used, and D017 showed that classifier is *better* (+0.039 to +0.110 at every
`k`). So a linear-arm win over `GreedyCover` would not be evidence about the paper's
algorithm.

**Proposal: the attention arm is the head-to-head result; the linear arm is a
robustness check on whether the search procedure itself (not the classifier) is what
matters.** `R` will label them that way and will not average them into one "H.1"
verdict. The human asked for both classifiers, which this satisfies — it is about
how the two are *reported*, not whether both run.

## F2 — Reusing `GreedyCover`/`JointGreedy` numbers requires one check D018 does not name

D018 says reuse them verbatim (I6) and that any discrepancy is a bug in this D's
reuse. **The specific risk is the one that bit D014 and D016:** D012/D013 store runs
only for γ values they *trained*, and D009/D010 hold the reused ones. `GreedyCover`
γ=0.1 is D009's `cvar_max`; `JointGreedy` γ=0.1 is D010's `joint_energy`.
**Proposal: assert the reused chains equal the ones stored in
`results/D008/selection.json` and `results/D010/selection.json` before any
comparison**, and assert the reused `k`=8 metrics equal D015's — the same cross-D
check that passed IDENTICAL in D016.

## F3 — Pre-registering the resolution problem, since it has bound every prior D

25 test configs, 4-pp granularity on worst-class, seed ranges of 0.024–0.081 on mean
top-1. D008/F3, D009, D010/R9, D011, D015/F3 and D017 each hit this. **A chain-to-chain
difference below ~0.03 on mean top-1 will not resolve**, and the PARITY-OR-BETTER
branch is defined by TOST at δ=0.02 — which is *inside* the attention network's own
seed range. **Proposal, fixed now:** for the attention arm, report PARITY only when
the TOST interval is within ±0.02 **and** |Δ| is below the 5-seed range; otherwise
INCONCLUSIVE. Without this, "parity" would be reported for a result the instrument
cannot distinguish from a loss.

## Failure handling

- **Wall-clock exceeds 1.5× the projection** — stop at the current `k` and report a
  partial chain; a nested greedy chain is valid at any prefix length.
- **A step's margin is below the seed range** — recorded per step, not treated as an
  error; it is the evidence for F3's caveat.
- **The linear arm's chain differs wildly from the attention arm's** — a finding
  (the search's result is classifier-dependent), reported under F1's labelling.
- **Cost guardrail:** stop past 8 GPU-hours total (1.7× the projection).
