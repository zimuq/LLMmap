## Part 0 — S1, and the thing it localises for free

Selection at the fine grid, both algorithms. **The first-picked query flips at
exactly two places**, and the flip points bracket γ=0.5 on one side:

| γ | 1.0 | 0.75 | 0.6 | **0.5** | 0.45 | 0.4 | 0.35 | 0.3 | 0.25 | 0.1 | 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| first query | 123 | **117** | 117 | **117** | **114** | 114 | 114 | 114 | 114 | **193** | 193 |
| CVaR pair count | 666 | 499 | 399 | 333 | 299 | 266 | 233 | 199 | 166 | 66 | 33 |

**γ=0.5 is not an isolated point — it sits in the middle of a three-γ family
{0.75, 0.6, 0.5} that all start from the same query, and the family boundary is
between 0.5 and 0.45.** That is exactly where a threshold would have to be if H1
is right, and S1 localises it without training anything.

**Identical `k`=8 sets** (so training them is replication, not new information):

| | groups |
|---|---|
| `GreedyCover` | {1.0} {0.75} **{0.6, 0.5}** **{0.45, 0.4, 0.35}** {0.3} {0.25} {0.1} {0.05} — 8 distinct |
| `JointGreedy` | {1.0} {0.75} {0.6} {0.5} **{0.45, 0.4, 0.35}** **{0.3, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05}** — 6 distinct |

Two consequences worth stating now:

1. **`GreedyCover` γ=0.6 and γ=0.5 select the identical `k`=8 set** (and identical
   at `k`=1, 2; differing only at `k`=3–7). D012/R4 measured the trained network's
   order-sensitivity at 0.0006 against a 0.0249 seed range, so **γ=0.6 at `k`=8 is
   a near-exact replication of D013's γ=0.5 result.** If γ=0.5's failure is chain
   luck, γ=0.6 must fail identically at `k`=8 — it is the same chain. That is a
   free, built-in check on H2 at one point.
2. **`JointGreedy` γ=0.3 now joins the {0.25, 0.1, 0.05} identical-set group**,
   extending D012/F2's collapse finding one grid point further.

**`JointGreedy`'s objective peak moves with γ, and the move is sharp:**

| γ | ≥ 0.4 | 0.35 | 0.3 / 0.25 | 0.1 / 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| peak `k` | **1** | 4 | 3 | 8 |

So D012/R2's "dimension-dominated past `k`=1" pathology is **not** a γ=1.0
property — it holds for **every γ ≥ 0.4**, and switches off between 0.4 and 0.35.
`GreedyCover`'s objective is monotone at **every** γ (checked, all 11). This is the
single most suggestive thing S1 produced and it is discussed in F2.

## F1 — D014's independence argument is weaker than stated, and the `k`=1 part of it is void

D014 rests the cross-algorithm test on: *"the two algorithms select **different**
chains for the same γ from `k`=2 on … 'Chain luck' is therefore independent across
algorithms."* Measured:

| γ | 1.0 | 0.75 | 0.6 | 0.5 | 0.45 | 0.4 | 0.35 | 0.3 | 0.25 | 0.1 | 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| same first query | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| `k`=4 overlap | 3/4 | 2/4 | 2/4 | 1/4 | 1/4 | 1/4 | 1/4 | 1/4 | 2/4 | 1/4 | 2/4 |
| `k`=8 overlap | 5/8 | 3/8 | 3/8 | 3/8 | 4/8 | 4/8 | 4/8 | 2/8 | 3/8 | 2/8 | 4/8 |

- **At `k`=1 the two algorithms are the same algorithm** (D010/R2, D013/F3), so
  they necessarily agree — the shared first-query flips in Part 0's table are **one
  observation, not two**, and cannot count as cross-algorithm corroboration.
- Beyond `k`=1 the chains are **partly** independent: 2–4 of 8 shared at `k`=8,
  1–2 of 4 at `k`=4. Not the same chain, but not independent draws either — they
  are two greedy walks over the **same frozen tensor**, so a query that is
  genuinely strong under a given γ-weighting tends to be picked by both.

**This does not invalidate the design — it is still the best available test — but
the strength of the inference should be stated honestly in `R`.** Agreement across
algorithms is *evidence against* chain luck, not a refutation of it, and the
`k`=1 row must be excluded from that evidence entirely. **Proposal: report the
cross-algorithm reading using `k` ≥ 2 only, with the overlap table above beside it
so a reader can weigh it.**

## F2 — The objective-peak transition is at 0.4/0.35 and the first-query flip is at 0.5/0.45. They do not coincide.

If a single mechanism produced a threshold in γ, the natural expectation is that
the selection's *character* and the objective's *behaviour* change together. They
do not: `JointGreedy`'s peak-`k` switches between **0.4 and 0.35**, while the
first-query family boundary is between **0.5 and 0.45**. Two different transitions,
one grid point apart.

That is worth pre-registering as a discriminator: **if training shows the
BENEFIT/NO-BENEFIT boundary at 0.5/0.45 (matching chain family) rather than at
0.4/0.35 (matching objective behaviour), then whatever γ does, it acts through
*which queries get picked first*, not through the objective's shape.** Stated now
so it cannot be constructed afterwards.

## F3 — My D013/R2 mechanism does not predict the observed sign, and I should retract it

D014's `## D` says so directly, and it is right. I wrote that `CVaR₀.₅` "commits to
neither mean nor tail" — that predicts performance **between** γ=1 and γ=0.25, but
D013 measured γ=0.5 at or slightly **below** γ=1.0 (worst-class: below γ=1.0 at 6
of 8 `k`). **A mechanism that predicts the wrong sign is not a weak explanation, it
is a wrong one.** `R` will retract it explicitly rather than let it stand as
background. I have no replacement mechanism to offer, and P1 does not invent one.

## Call 1 (design side) — the grid and the labelling rule, fixed before training

**Grid — as D014 proposed, unchanged:** γ ∈ {1.0, 0.75, 0.6, 0.5, 0.45, 0.4, 0.35,
0.3, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05}. It satisfies D014's constraints (points both sides of 0.5 at
close spacing; one point ≥ 0.75) and S1 shows the family boundary falls inside it.

**Labelling rule, per algorithm, per γ, against γ=1.0, on mean top-1** — reusing
D012/D013's machinery unchanged:

- Per `k`, a γ is **better** if its bootstrap CI excludes 0 **and** |Δ| exceeds the
  5-seed run range (D009/F1's rule); **equivalent** if TOST at δ=0.02 returns
  equivalence; **unresolved** otherwise.
- Over `k`=1..8, the γ is labelled:
  - **BENEFIT** — better at **≥ 5 of 8** `k`, and never worse at any `k`.
  - **NO-BENEFIT** — better at **≤ 1 of 8** `k`.
  - **UNRESOLVED** — anything else.

The 5/8 and 1/8 cut-points are chosen to match how the existing data already
labels itself: D013's γ=0.1 (6/8 exceeding range) and γ=0.05 (7/8) are BENEFIT;
D013's γ=0.5 (0/8) is NO-BENEFIT; D013's γ=0.25 (5/8) is BENEFIT at exactly the
boundary. **Those three labels are therefore fixed by data that already exists, and
the rule is calibrated to reproduce them — which is the point of fixing it now.**

## Call 2 (design side) — Arm B scope

**Proposal: R = 30 half-subsamples, γ ∈ {1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.35, 0.25, 0.1}, both
algorithms; train nothing from Arm B.**

- **R = 30** gives a usable IQR and a 5th/95th band; D008/R5 used 2 halves and
  D010/S6b used 2 — 30 is the first time this project has a real distribution
  rather than a spread of two.
- **Sampling without replacement**, 37 of 75 build configs, as D014 specifies.
- **Proxy only, no training from Arm B.** D014's Call 2 asks which resampled chains
  to train; my answer is **none**. Arm B's question is *"is the γ=0.5 chain
  unusually bad among chains that γ=0.5 could have produced"* — a within-γ,
  within-algorithm ranking, which is exactly what D009/R2 licensed the proxy for
  (Spearman 0.95–0.98 on ranking). Training 180 extra chains to re-establish a
  ranking the proxy already gives would cost ~4× Arm A for no new inference.
  **If design side wants a training check, the cheapest sufficient one is the two
  extreme chains (best and worst resampled) at γ=0.5 for `GreedyCover`, 10 runs —
  say so and I will add it.**
- Arm B never touches val/test (I2): selection sees only a subset of build, and
  evaluation uses the **full** build split as the 1-NN reference.
