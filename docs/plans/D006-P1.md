## F1 — BLOCKING: **C7 is still open, and D006 freezes it into the corpus irreversibly**

D006 mentions `C7` nowhere. Its only reference to the token budget is the cost
line "batch-8, 100–200 tok", which treats the choice as a costing *range*. But
generation cannot be run over a range — S4 must pick one, and whichever it picks
is baked into all 1,077,625 responses.

This collides with two invariants D006 itself invokes:

- **I6**: all later selection-algorithm comparisons must reuse this corpus, never
  regenerate.
- **I7**'s stated rationale, verbatim from `CLAUDE.md:111`: *"The corpus costs
  ~10⁵ generations; regenerating it over a renamed field is the most expensive
  avoidable mistake here."*

`DECISIONS.md` C7 is ⬜ open and says explicitly: *"Decide C7 on information
grounds, not cost — still open."* D006 would resolve it by default, silently, at
whatever `llm.py:9` happens to say (`max_new_tokens = 100`).

### This is not hypothetical — the current cap censors ~38% of responses

Measured on the shipped corpus (62,394 responses, `data/datasets/default_dataset.jsonl`),
using "response does not end on terminal punctuation" as a truncation proxy:

| response length (chars) | share ending mid-sentence |
|---|---|
| 0–99 | 10.7% |
| 100–199 | 4.1% |
| 200–299 | 4.2% |
| 300–399 | 23.8% |
| **400–499** | **66.5%** |
| **500–599** | **83.4%** |
| **600–699** | **91.2%** |

The proxy's own false-positive rate is visible in the 100–299 band: **~4%** is the
stylistic baseline (responses legitimately ending in code, lists, non-Latin
punctuation). The monotone climb to 83–91% above 500 chars is not style — it is
right-censoring at `max_new_tokens=100`. Overall 41.9% of responses are
unterminated, so **~38 percentage points is truncation**.

### Why this project in particular cannot wave that through

**D004 is the precedent.** D001's negative half was produced by a bounded,
ceiling-pinned statistic; D004 showed the same data gave M6 = 0.997 (censored) vs
**0.616** (uncensored energy distance). The finding that survived was: *a
statistic computed on a censored variable measures the censoring, not the
population.* Generating the frozen Phase-1 corpus under a cap that truncates 38%
of it applies that same hazard at the level of the **raw material**, where no
later choice of statistic can undo it.

Concretely, plausible fingerprint signal that a 100-token cap destroys: response
length itself, sign-off and closing style, whether a model hedges at the end,
refusal length, and whether an enumerated answer completes. A model that would
write 300 tokens and one that would write 130 are **identical** under the cap.

### Proposal (AMENDED 2026-09-05) — C7 does not have to be a freeze-point decision at all

My first draft of this section proposed a pilot to inform C7 before generating.
That is still worth doing, but while writing up the trade-off for the human I
realised the framing was wrong, and the better answer removes most of the risk
instead of measuring it.

**`max_new_tokens` is a stopping rule, not a generation parameter.** Under greedy
or ancestral sampling, token *t*'s distribution depends only on tokens `< t`; the
cap only decides when decoding halts. So the first 100 tokens of a 200-token
sample are distributed exactly as a 100-token sample would be — EOS handling
included, since a response that naturally ends at token 50 ends there under either
cap.

**Therefore: generate once at the higher cap, store the full text, and C7 becomes a
post-hoc analysis parameter.** Truncating stored 200-token responses down to 100
reproduces the 100-token corpus exactly. The reverse is impossible.

This changes what C7 *is*. It is no longer "an irreversible choice frozen into the
corpus by I6/I7" but "the ceiling we pay to generate up to", with every value below
it recoverable for free, forever, without regeneration. The only irreversible part
is the ceiling itself.

**Revised recommendation: generate the corpus at 200 tokens, and record C7 in the
manifest as the *generation ceiling*, with the analysis-time budget left free
below it.** Cost 199–240 node-h (2.9–3.5% of the 6869 SU balance), makespan
10.7–13.0 h, storage ~0.8 GB instead of ~0.4 GB. In exchange, the 38%-censoring
hazard cannot be baked in, and if later analysis wants 100 tokens for
comparability, it is a slice, not a re-run.

I do **not** recommend pushing the ceiling to 400: cost roughly doubles again
(~400 node-h, ~21 h makespan, past this plan's own proposed guardrail) for
headroom the pilot can tell us we probably do not need.

**The pilot is still worth running, and now answers a sharper question.** One
generation pass at 400 tokens over ~6 models spanning 0.5B–14B (~1,200
generations, ~30 min plus load), analysed at 100 / 200 / 400 by truncation — one
pass, three caps, because of the same property above. It reports:

1. **Censoring at each cap** — is 200 an actual ceiling, or just a higher wall?
2. **Whether separability improves from 100 → 200 → 400** under the frozen I5
   embedding. This is the half my first draft missed, and it is the half that
   `DECISIONS.md` C7's "decide on information grounds" actually asks for.

Point 2 matters because **more tokens might make separability worse, not better** —
see the honest-risk note below. Six models gives 15 pairs, enough to see a
direction, not enough for a CI; it is a sanity check on the ceiling choice, not a
finding.

### Honest risks in raising the cap (recorded so review can weigh them)

- **Dilution.** Mean-pooled sentence embeddings average over tokens. If the
  fingerprint signal is concentrated in a response's *opening* (greeting style,
  immediate refusal, formatting choice), appending 100 more tokens of generic
  continuation pulls every model toward a common centroid and *reduces* `Sep`.
  This is a real possibility, not a hedge — hence pilot question 2.
- **e5 context window.** `multilingual-e5-large-instruct` is trained on
  passage-length text with a 512-token limit. 200 tokens of response plus the
  query is comfortably inside it; 400 begins to crowd it. Another reason not to
  raise the ceiling further.
- **Degenerate repetition.** Small models at high temperature loop. At 100 tokens
  a loop is barely visible; at 400 it can dominate the embedding, producing
  apparent separability that is really "which models loop" — a genuine model
  property, but one that would swamp subtler signal.
- **Comparability with the paper.** LLMmap's shipped corpus is at 100 tokens.
  Raising the cap is one more difference — though comparability is already gone by
  design (different embedding model, query pool, and universe), so this is
  bookkeeping rather than a cost.

**Verification this rests on, to be checked in S1, not assumed:** generate greedily
at 200 and at 100 on the same prompts; the 100-token output must be a
**character-exact prefix** of the 200-token output. If that fails, the truncation
argument is void for this stack and C7 reverts to a genuine freeze-point decision.
Cheap, and it belongs in S1's existing batching-verification pass either way.

**This is still a Part-C decision and still design side's to make.** What has
changed is that a wrong answer is now cheap to correct in one direction and only
one direction — which is the argument for setting the ceiling at 200.

## F2 — BLOCKING: **C4 is a three-way split; the code and configs support two pools**

S3 says to construct `S_build`/`S_val`/`S_test` "per C4 (75/25/25, decided), using
D005's now-fixed `PromptConfFactory`", and to reuse D005's disjointness test
against the result. That reads as though the machinery is ready. It is not.

Verified:

- `confs/prompt_configurations/train_test_split.json` has exactly **two** top-level
  keys, `train` and `test`. There is no `val` pool and no third partition of
  anything.
- `prompt_configuration.py:6` defines `TRAIN, TEST = 'train', 'test'` — two
  constants. `_cond_choice` (`:256`) indexes `self.train_test_split[pool]`, so a
  third pool simply raises `KeyError`.
- D005's `sampling_universe_split` (`general.json`) likewise has two keys, and
  D005's `test_i2_disjointness.py` iterates `for pool in (TRAIN, TEST)`.

**D005 fixed a two-pool split. C4 requires three.** The extension is not
mechanical, because the collections are thin:

| collection | total | current 2-way | 3-way would be |
|---|---|---|---|
| `systems` | 84 | 51 / 33 | ~42 / 21 / 21 — fine |
| `temperature` (distinct) | 10 | 5 / 5 | 4 / 3 / 3 — workable |
| `cot_prompts` | **6** | 4 / 2 | **2 / 2 / 2** |
| `rag_prompts` | **9** | 6 / 3 | **3 / 3 / 3** |
| `do_sample` | 2 | shared (A5) | shared (A5) |

The bottom two rows re-open **exactly the A5 question**. With `COT_P = 0.2`, a
25-config `S_val` contains ~5 CoT-bearing configs drawn from a 2-element pool — so
"val's chain-of-thought behaviour" would be determined by which 2 of 6 prompts
landed there. That is not a holdout; it is a coin flip with a variance penalty,
and it is the same "worse confound than the leakage it removes" reasoning that
carried A5.

### Call 3 (new) — how should the three-way split be constructed?

Three options, with the trade stated rather than buried:

1. **Full 3-way disjoint on all collections.** Maximally faithful to I2's literal
   wording. Cost: `cot`/`rag` holdouts of 2–3 elements, high variance, and val/test
   wrapper behaviour becomes an artifact of the split draw.
2. **3-way on `systems` + `temperature`; `cot_prompts` and `rag_prompts` shared
   across all three pools**, as an explicit A5-style documented carve-out. Cost:
   genuine wrapper leakage build→val→test.
3. **(recommended)** **3-way on `systems` + `temperature`; `cot`/`rag` split
   *two* ways — `build`-disjoint, with `val` and `test` sharing the holdout half**
   (`cot` 3/3, `rag` 5/4). Rationale: the leak I2 exists to prevent is
   *selection-on-test*, i.e. `build` seeing what the evaluation pools see. A
   `val`↔`test` overlap is a milder over-fitting concern, not a validity failure,
   and it buys pools of 3–5 wrappers instead of 2.

I recommend **(3)** but this is a Part-C-shaped call about how far I2 bends, and
A5 established that such calls are design side's. I will implement whichever is
approved; I will not pick one unilaterally.

Whatever is chosen, the schema change to `train_test_split.json` and
`general.json` is **an I7 event** (a config file the code reads, changing shape) —
version bump + migration note before generation, per I7.

## F3 — A **third** silent defect in `sample()`, same class as D005's two

While checking S3's machinery I found that `PromptConfFactory.sample()`'s
uniqueness guarantee does not hold. Its docstring says *"Sample n unique confs
from `pool`"* and it is implemented as:

```python
s = set()
while len(s) != n:
    s.add(self.sample_one(pool))
```

`PromptConf` defines neither `__eq__` nor `__hash__` (verified: both absent from
`PromptConf.__dict__`). Python therefore falls back to identity hashing, every
freshly-constructed object is unique to the `set`, no `add` is ever rejected, and
the loop terminates after exactly `n` iterations having deduplicated **nothing**.

**Measured over 40 seeds, at C4's actual sizes:**

- `S_build` (n=75): mean **3.95** duplicate configs, max 8 → ~5% of the build
  split is redundant.
- `S_val`/`S_test` (n=25): mean 0.55 duplicates.
- **33% of all duplicates land in the "bare" cell** (no system prompt, no CoT, no
  RAG) — a cell that is only **6.2%** of draws. Duplicates are not spread evenly;
  they concentrate in the configuration with the fewest distinguishing
  dimensions, because once the three prompt slots are empty only ~6 distinct
  sampling behaviours remain.

This is the same failure mode as D005's two defects — a guarantee asserted in the
code and silently unenforced — in the same function, and it survived D005 because
D005 was scoped to pool-correctness, not uniqueness. It does **not** invalidate
D005: the disjointness test checks which *indices* are drawn per pool, which
duplicates do not affect.

Impact if unfixed: `S_build` is effectively ~71 distinct configs rather than 75,
with the redundancy concentrated in the least informative cell, and the corpus's
per-model point cloud over-weights bare prompts. Not fatal — but it is baked in by
I6 and free to fix now.

### Call 4 (new) — what is the dedup key?

The fix is small; the *key* is a judgment call:

- **(recommended)** `(raw, sorted(sampling_hparams))` — the design point. Note
  `rag_prompt` is *materialised* with a randomly chosen document at construction
  time (`_generate_rag_prompt`, `:200`), so keying on the materialised string
  would essentially never collide and would leave the bug in place under a new
  name. Keying on `raw` treats the document draw as realisation, not design.
- Alternative: also treat `do_sample=False` configs as equivalent regardless of
  `temperature` (greedy decoding ignores temperature, so those configs are
  behaviourally identical but currently counted as distinct). This is arguably
  more correct and slightly more aggressive; flagging rather than assuming.

Implementation is `__eq__`/`__hash__` on `PromptConf` plus a retry cap so a pool
too small to yield `n` distinct configs raises instead of spinning forever.
**Shared-module change → disclosed here, not folded in silently**, same standard
as D005.

## F4 — The wall-clock guardrail is ~2× optimistic and will fire a false alarm

D006's budget guardrail says *"~4–6 hours wall-clock across ~2 waves of ≤20
concurrent jobs"*, and instructs me to stop and report if actual cost runs
"meaningfully past this". At 4–6 h that instruction fires on a **healthy** run.

The sharding unit is one model, so the makespan is `ceil(37/20) = 2` rounds of a
full per-model shard, not total-node-hours ÷ 20:

- per-model shard = 233 × 125 = **29,125 generations**
- at D002's measured batch-8 rate (2.255–2.478 gen/s @ 100 tok): **3.3–3.6 h per
  shard** → makespan **6.5–7.2 h**
- at 200 tok (1.246–1.506 gen/s): **5.4–6.5 h per shard** → makespan
  **10.7–13.0 h**

Even the naive `node-hours / 20` gives 6.0–6.6 h (100 tok) and 9.9–12.0 h (200
tok) — so 4–6 h is below both readings. **I believe this figure is mine
originally**: in my 2026-09-04 estimate I divided 85 node-hours by 20 slots to get
~4.3 h, which is a *utilisation* number, not a makespan — with model-granular
shards, 20 finish at 3.4 h and the remaining ones only at 6.8 h. Design side
carried it forward in good faith and rescaled the node-hours to 37 models without
the makespan inheriting the same rescale. Correcting it here.

Two things this omits, both pushing the same direction:

- **~478 GB of model weights must be fetched** across the 37 shards. Shards run in
  parallel but share one network path; budget download/load separately from
  generation rather than assuming it is free.
- D002 measured throughput on **3B and 7B only**. Its own R2 finding — *"throughput
  does not track parameter count"*, batch-8 still being launch-latency-bound — is
  the reason it is fair to treat the rate as flat, but the universe spans
  **0.5B–14B** and `Phi-3-medium-128k` (14B) is 2× the largest model measured.
  Treating the rate as flat there is an extrapolation, and I will state it as one
  in R rather than let it pass as measurement.

**Proposed replacement guardrail:** stop-and-report if wall-clock exceeds **20 h**
or SU spend exceeds **350 node-hours**. Both are ~1.6× the honest estimate, which
is what a guardrail should be — wide enough not to trip on variance, tight enough
to catch an order-of-magnitude error.

## F5 — The guardrail should be per-query, because S2 grows `Q_0`

The 126–218 node-hour figure is computed on `|Q_0| = 233`, but S2 *adds*
tokenizer probes to that pool. At ~20–40 new entries the corpus grows 9–17% and
the cost grows with it. Minor, but a guardrail stated on a number the plan itself
changes is a guardrail that mis-fires. Cost should be quoted as
**~0.52–0.57 node-hours per query at 100 tok** (0.85–1.03 at 200 tok) and
multiplied by the final `|Q_0|` once S2 closes.

## F6 — The manifest should pin model revisions, or I6 is not actually enforceable

S5's manifest spec lists schema version, model list, generation date, and shard
completeness. It does not pin **which weights** were used. `llm.py:38` calls
`from_pretrained(llm_name, ...)` with no `revision=`, so it resolves whatever
`main` points to on the Hub that day. Model repos do get updated in place.

I6 says every later comparison reuses the same frozen corpus — which holds — but
any future partial regeneration (a re-run of one failed shard, a later addition)
would silently mix weight versions. Recording the resolved commit SHA per model
costs nothing at load time and is unrecoverable afterwards. Folded into Call 2's
manifest proposal below.

## Call 1 (D006's own) — tokenizer probes

D006 asks for ~5–10 concrete instances per category, noting the paper flags glitch
tokens as unexplored (§5.1) so there is no baseline to transcribe. Proposed, to be
tagged `tokenizer-probe-<category>` alongside D003's `anchor` / `generated-<family>`
provenance scheme:

**`digit-chunking`** (tokenizers differ: per-digit vs 3-digit groups vs BPE merges)
1. "What is 987654321 plus 123456789? Give only the number."
2. "How many digits does 1000000 have?"
3. "Write 12345678901234567890 with commas as thousands separators."
4. "Reverse the digits of 9081726354."
5. "Is 4831 greater than 4813? Answer yes or no, then restate both numbers."
6. "Count from 998 to 1003, one number per line."
7. "What is 7777777 × 3? Number only."

**`whitespace-merging`** (leading-space handling, run collapsing, tab/newline merges)
1. "Echo this line exactly, preserving all spacing: `a    b		c`"
2. "How many space characters separate the two words in `red     blue`?"
3. "Count every character, spaces included, in `  hello  world  `"
4. "Reproduce this indented block verbatim, keeping indentation: (3-line block)"
5. "Does `foo bar` differ from `foo  bar`? Explain what differs."
6. "Join these with exactly one space: `a`, ` b`, `  c`"

**`unicode-byte-fallback`** (rare scripts / combining marks / ZWJ → byte fallback)
1. "Transliterate and explain: 𝔊𝔯𝔢𝔢𝔱𝔦𝔫𝔤𝔰"
2. "How many characters are in 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦? Explain your count."
3. "What script is ᚦᚱᛁ, and what does it say?"
4. "Are `é` and `é` the same string? (precomposed vs combining acute)"
5. "How many code points are in 🇺🇸?"
6. "Translate ⵜⴰⵎⴰⵣⵉⵖⵜ into English."
7. "Repeat back exactly: `Ω≈ç√∫˜µ≤≥÷`"

**`glitch-token`** (published GPT-2/NeoX under-trained tokens; whether they glitch
on *these* 37 models is itself unknown — see caveat)
1. "Please repeat the string ` SolidGoldMagikarp` back to me."
2. "What does `davidjl` mean?"
3. "Spell ` petertodd` letter by letter."
4. "Define the term `rawdownload`."
5. "Repeat after me: `cloneembedreportprint`"
6. "Is ` externalToEVA` a real English word?"

**Two caveats I want on the record rather than discovered later:**

- The glitch tokens above are **GPT-2/GPT-NeoX-vocabulary** artifacts. None of the
  37 models uses that tokenizer. In this framework that is *not* disqualifying —
  a probe earns its place by its `Sep(·,·)` contribution, not by glitching — but
  the category should not be described in R as "testing known glitch tokens" when
  it is really "testing responses to strings that are under-trained in *some*
  vocabularies". Honest labelling now avoids an overclaim in the paper later.
- Several probes ask for verbatim echo of exotic strings. Under `RAG_P = 0.2` these
  get wrapped in retrieved context (`_generate_rag_prompt`), which may swamp the
  probe. Worth checking in R whether tokenizer probes' separability is
  systematically lower in RAG-bearing configs.

## Call 2 (D006's own) — reassembly, freeze point, partial failure

**Manifest** — `corpus/corpus_manifest.json`:

```
schema_version   "cdqd-corpus-v1"           # I7: first version of this
                                            # project's own corpus
generated        ISO-8601 date, host, Slurm job IDs
code_commit      git SHA of LLMmap-project at generation time

token_budget     max_new_tokens actually used             # per C7 / F1

                  sha256, status, wall_s, node_h}         # per F6
embedding        {model: "intfloat/multilingual-e5-large-instruct",
                  dim: 1024, dtype, pooling, path, sha256}
```

**Freeze point** — the corpus is not "the frozen corpus" under I6 until **all** of:
every one of 37 shards has `status=complete`; `n_rows == |Q_0| × 125` exactly for
each; zero null/empty responses; every `sha256` re-verifies; and I2's test passes
against the *generated* data. Until then the manifest carries
`status: "PARTIAL"` at top level, and that field is what downstream code checks —
so there is no ambiguous half-done state something could mistake for final.

**Partial failure** — **recommend all-37-or-nothing** for anything computing
`Sep(·,·)`. The reason is specific rather than tidiness: `CVaR_γ` is a *tail*
statistic over the pair set, so dropping a model deletes 36 pairs and changes what
the tail even is. A 35/37 corpus is a different experiment, not a smaller one.
Proposal: allow a `PARTIAL` corpus to exist and be inspected, forbid it from being
consumed, and let per-model retries happen out of band without touching the
`READY` declaration.

## Steps

Ordering reflects the blocks: **S1 and S2 can run now; S3 onward cannot.**

**S0 — Bring-up (fresh session).** **`conda activate llmmap-gpu`** (NOT `llmmap` — that env is `torch 2.7.1+cpu`; see `docs/ENV.md`);
`export HF_MODEL_CACHE=$SCRATCH/hf-cache` and `HF_HOME=$SCRATCH/hf-cache`;
confirm `HUGGINGFACE_API_KEY` is exported. Never on the login node (`TACC_NOTES.md`).

**S1 — Fix `dataset_maker.py`'s batch-1 loop + verify + C7 pilot.** *(runnable now)*
`LLM_huggingface.generate` (`llm.py:64-89`) already accepts a list: it tokenizes
with `padding=True`, the tokenizer is constructed `padding_side='left'` (`:30`),
and the output slice `out_toks[i, in_toks.input_ids[i].shape[0]:]` is correct
precisely *because* padding is left-side. So the change is confined to
`make_dataset_entries_for_new_llm` (`dataset_maker.py:32-41`): replace the
per-query loop with chunked batches of B queries sharing one config's
`sample_params`. Exact diff disclosed in `## P` before execution.

Verification, per D006's "numerically, not just runs-without-error":
- **Greedy exact-match** (`do_sample=False`): batched output must be
  **character-identical** to unbatched over ≥200 (query, config) pairs on ≥2
  models. This is the test that actually catches padding/attention-mask errors —
  any left-padding bug shows up as a greedy mismatch.
- **Stochastic distributional check** (`do_sample=True`): exact match is
  impossible (batching changes RNG consumption), so compare length distribution
  and I5-embedding centroid/spread, batched vs unbatched, ≥500 generations.
- Sweep B ∈ {1, 8, 16, 32} for throughput and peak memory; pick the largest B that
  stays under ~80 GB on the 14B model.
- **Prefix-exactness check (F1's amended proposal rests on this):** greedy
  generation at 200 tokens, truncated to 100, must be a **character-exact prefix**
  of greedy generation at 100 on the same prompts. If it is not, the
  "generate high, truncate down" argument is void for this stack and C7 reverts to
  a genuine freeze-point decision. Report either way.
- **C7 pilot (F1, amended):** **one** generation pass at 400 tokens over ~6 models
  spanning 0.5B–14B (~1,200 generations, ~30 min + load), analysed at 100/200/400
  **by truncation** — one pass covers all three caps by the same property. Reports
  (a) censoring rate per cap and (b) whether `Sep` under the frozen I5 embedding

## Failure handling

- **A model won't load on aarch64 / gated repo.** Retry once; then record the shard
  `failed` with the exception and continue the other 36. Do **not** substitute a
  different model — A1 is the anchor. Report in R; the all-37-or-nothing rule
  (Call 2) then decides whether the corpus can close.
- **OOM at chosen batch size.** Fall back to the next smaller B for that shard and
  record the per-shard B in the manifest, since throughput claims depend on it.
- **Greedy exact-match fails in S1.** Stop. Do not proceed to S4 on a batching
  path that changes outputs — that would silently corrupt the whole corpus. Report
  as a blocker.
- **Wall-clock or SU past F4's replacement guardrail (20 h / 350 node-h).** Stop
  and report rather than spending through it.
- **`$SCRATCH` purge.** At ≤13 h makespan this is not close to the 10-day window,
  but weights are re-downloadable by design; corpus and embeddings go to `$WORK`,
  which is not purged.
