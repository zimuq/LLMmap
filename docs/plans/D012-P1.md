## Part 0 — The hypothesis is testable before training, and it fails

D012's `## D` pre-registers: *"mean-aggregation might already implicitly favor
hard pairs for the joint statistic … making γ less necessary for `JointGreedy`."*
S4 infers this indirectly from objective traces. It can be measured **directly**:
under each chain, where do the 65 near-relative pairs sit in the per-pair joint
statistic?

| chain | tail (CVaR₀.₁) | mean | hard-pair share of worst decile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **γ = 1.0** | **0.1555** | **0.8788** | 0.15 |
| γ = 0.25 / 0.1 / 0.05 | **0.2434** | 0.5703 | 0.33 |

(base rate of near-relative pairs: 65/666 = **9.8%**)

**Mean-aggregation does not already capture the tail.** Going from γ=1 to γ<1
lifts the worst-decile mean by **+56%** (0.1555 → 0.2434) and more than doubles
the hard-pair concentration in that decile (0.15 → 0.33). The hypothesis predicted
γ would matter *less* for `JointGreedy`; at the selection-objective level it
matters **a great deal**, and in the ordinary direction.

**And the trade-off D008 did not find for `GreedyCover`, `JointGreedy` has.**
D008/R6 reported *"no visible mean-vs-tail trade-off — the tail-weighted objective
is also the better mean objective."* Here the mean **falls 35%** (0.879 → 0.570)
to buy that tail. This is a genuine difference in the two algorithms' γ-behaviour,
visible without training a single network.

**Caveat that keeps this honest:** this is the *selection objective*, not accuracy.
D010/R4 found the joint objective and the trained outcome diverge in a way this
project has not explained. A large tensor-level trade-off may or may not survive
to trained accuracy — that is exactly what S5 measures, and P1 does **not**
pre-judge it. What Part 0 settles is the *mechanism* claim in `## D`, which is a
statement about the statistic and is therefore answerable at the statistic's level.

## F1 — The γ=1.0 objective peaks at `k=1` and declines monotonically thereafter

| `k` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| γ=1.0 | **1.3025** | 1.1092 | 1.0502 | 0.9953 | 0.9493 | 0.9170 | 0.8962 | 0.8788 |
| γ=0.05 | 0.1456 | 0.1959 | 0.1978 | 0.1982 | 0.1974 | 0.2007 | 0.2048 | **0.2053** |

D010/F2 flagged that this objective is not monotone in `k` (each query adds 1024
dimensions and the value can fall); D010/R3 found it *did not bind* for γ=0.1.
**At γ=1.0 it binds completely** — every single query after the first makes the
mean joint statistic worse, so a greedy maximising it is adding queries it
"believes" are harmful. Peak `k`: **1 (γ=1.0), 3 (γ=0.25), 8 (γ=0.1 and 0.05)**.

This matters for interpreting S5: if γ=1.0 underperforms in trained accuracy, the
reading is *not* simply "CVaR helps" — it may be "the mean joint objective is
dimension-dominated and stops being a sensible selection criterion past `k=1`."
Those are different findings and `R` will distinguish them, using the trace above
as the discriminator.

## F2 — At `k=8` three of the four grid points select the **identical set**

| pair of γ | set identity at `k` = 1, 2, 3–7, 8 |
|---|---|
| 0.1 vs 0.05 | same, same, **differ**, **same** |
| 0.25 vs 0.1 | differ, differ, differ, **same** |
| 0.25 vs 0.05 | differ, differ, differ, **same** |

At `k=8`, γ ∈ {0.25, 0.1, 0.05} all select `{0, 16, 114, 117, 140, 193, 233, 237}`
— differing only in selection *order* — with byte-identical per-pair statistics
(CVaR₀.₁ 0.2434, mean 0.5703 for all three).

**Consequence for the outcome table:** at `k=8`, the grid has **two** distinct
conditions, not four. A `k=8`-only comparison across γ cannot discriminate among
the sub-1 values even in principle. At `k ≤ 7` they do differ, so the full
`k=1..8` grid remains worth running — but the headline `k=8` row must be reported
as *one* sub-1 condition, not three, and the three trained `k=8` results should be
pooled as five-seed replicates of the same selection rather than presented as a
γ-trend.

**Free bonus this creates:** the three `k=8` chains are the same set in different
slot order, so training them measures **how much the trained network depends on
query order within a fixed set** — never measured in this project, and relevant
because `d009_lib.build_traces` assigns slots positionally. Reported as its own
line in `R` rather than absorbed.

## F3 — D008's γ answer for `GreedyCover` is proxy-only, and weaker than `## D` states

`## D` says *"D008 already answered it for `GreedyCover`: lower γ cost nothing on
mean accuracy and helped worst-class at small `k`."* Two corrections:

1. **D008's γ sweep was never trained.** `results/D008/gamma_sweep.json` is the
   1-NN proxy over frozen embeddings; D009 trained only four conditions
   (`cvar_max`, `mean_greedy_max`, `paper8`, `random`) and no γ variants.
   **So S6's request — report the trade-off curve "the same way D008/T2.2 did, for
   direct comparability across the two algorithms' γ-sensitivity" — would compare a
   trained curve against a proxy one.** D009 established the proxy ranks well
   (Spearman 0.95–0.98) but shifts *levels* unevenly by condition (+0.043 to
   +0.105), so a cross-algorithm γ-sensitivity claim built on that is confounded.
   **Proposal: report both `JointGreedy` curves — trained (new) and proxy
   (computable free from the frozen tensor) — and compare `GreedyCover`-vs-
   `JointGreedy` γ-sensitivity only on the proxy-to-proxy pair**, stating the
   trained curve as this D's own result without a cross-algorithm claim attached.
2. **"Helped worst-class" is not resolved even on the proxy.** D008's own
   bootstrap CIs at `k=8`: γ=1.0 → 0.235 [0.120, 0.360]; γ=0.05 → 0.345 [0.200,
   0.480] — heavily overlapping. And γ=0.5 is **non-monotone** on mean top-1
   (0.636 at `k=3` against γ=1.0's 0.697), which is what selection noise looks
   like. D008's own R6 said the sweep *"does not separate"* 0.05 from 0.1.

Logged to `TACC_NOTES` (`## D` is design-side owned). **Not blocking.**

## F4 — NULL is unreachable as written; it needs an equivalence test

The outcome table's NULL branch requires all four γ *"perform statistically
indistinguishably across all four metrics, at every `k`"*, and INCONCLUSIVE is
*"mixed or noisy signal."* As written these are not distinguishable: with the
5-seed range at 0.024–0.081 on mean top-1 and ±0.20 CIs on worst-class, *every*
result will be "indistinguishable" **and** "noisy". The D itself names this wall
(*"the same resolution wall D008/D009/D010/D011 have each hit"*) and asks me to
separate NULL from INCONCLUSIVE by whether values *"look genuinely similar, not
just noisy"* — which needs an operational definition, chosen before the numbers
exist.

**Proposal — a two-sided equivalence test (TOST), pre-registered:**

- **δ = 0.02 on mean top-1** — the smallest difference this project has ever
  resolved (D010's `k=8` CVaR-vs-paper8 was +0.031 with a 0.038 run range), so
  anything under it is below this instrument's demonstrated floor.
- **NULL**: the 95% CI of the γ-to-γ difference lies **entirely within ±δ**. That
  is positive evidence of equivalence, not absence of evidence.
- **CONFIRMED**: the CI **excludes 0** *and* |Δ| exceeds the 5-seed run range, at
  one or more `k` — D009/F1's rule, unchanged.
- **INCONCLUSIVE**: neither — the CI spans both 0 and ±δ, i.e. underpowered.

Without this, "NULL" would be reported for a result that is merely unresolved,
which is the exact error D001 made with censoring and D008 made with its oracle.
