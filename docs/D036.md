## Why this D

- D035 (v2) found CLOUD − CENTROID-raw **WORSE** under attention (−1.8 pp)
  and level under linear. D024 (v1) had found +1.5 pp.
- **Both compared two things at once.** CLOUD's statistic (D010, scale-free)
  is the raw joint energy distance **divided by the pair's mean within-cloud
  distance**:
  `(2·E‖X−Y‖ − E‖X−X′‖ − E‖Y−Y′‖) / ((E‖X−X′‖ + E‖Y−Y′‖)/2)`
  (`LLMmap/joint_greedy.py:_stat_all_pairs`, `experiments/d010_lib.py:69`).
  CENTROID-raw, `‖μ_v − μ_v′‖` over the concatenated queries, has no such
  division. So "distribution vs mean" was confounded with "per-pair scaling
  vs none".
- Scaling is not a small factor here:
  - CENTROID-raw beat CENTROID-norm by 8.7 pp (D035);
  - in D033's open-set readout ②, the raw variant beat the scale-free one
    by 15–19 pp O_all on S_val.
- **CLOUD-raw** (the same JG on the undivided raw joint energy) separates
  the two factors. It is point cloud vs point cloud, so it is I3-compliant.
- D010 chose scale-free for cross-`k` comparability. JG's greedy only
  compares candidate sets of the same size at each step, and CVaR runs over
  pairs at fixed `k`, so the raw statistic is a valid JG objective.

## D — The question

> **On corpus v2, with JointGreedy-CVaR₀.₁ held fixed, does a chain selected
> on the raw (undivided) joint energy distance between point clouds train to
> higher accuracy than (P1) a chain selected on raw centroid distance, and
> (P2) the chain selected on the scale-free joint energy (the method's
> current statistic)?**

### Arms

| arm | statistic between `v`, `v′` over the running set (concatenated queries) | status |
|---|---|---|
| **CLOUD-raw** | `2·E‖X−Y‖ − E‖X−X′‖ − E‖Y−Y′‖`, the 75 S_build points per model (V-statistic, same accumulators as D010 / D029) | **new** |
| **CENTROID-raw** | `‖μ_v − μ_v′‖` | **D035's chain and counts, verbatim** |
| **CLOUD** (scale-free) | the CLOUD-raw numerator divided by `(E‖X−X′‖ + E‖Y−Y′‖)/2` | **D029's chain and counts, verbatim** |
| PAPER8 | LLMmap's 8 queries | reference row only (existing counts) |

- **Selection for CLOUD-raw:**
  - greedy, maximising CVaR₀.₁ over the 3,570 pairs (`m` = 357, `cvar()`);
  - nested `k` = 1..8; candidates scanned in ascending id, ties to the
    lowest id;
  - same greedy as `d024_lib.greedy_cvar`, with hooks identical to
    `cloud_hooks` except that the per-pair statistic returns the raw
    numerator instead of the ratio.
  - The within-cloud terms are the same diagonal-inclusive means the
    scale-free statistic uses.
- **Code-path checks:**
  - the new hooks with the ratio restored must reproduce D029's chain with
    per-step objective diff 0, as D035 S0 did;
  - the raw statistic for one set must match `d010_lib.joint_energy_direct`
    (`raw`) from explicit concatenated clouds, within float32 tolerance, on
    a few (pair, set) cases.
- **No new code in existing files.** The hooks go in a new `d036_*` module;
  `LLMmap/joint_greedy.py` and `d024_lib.py` / `d035_lib.py` are imported,
  not edited.
- **No variant choice.** Every arm's statistic is fixed above, so S_val is
  used only for early stopping. Unlike D035, there is no S_val selection
  step.

### What each comparison isolates

- **P1 CLOUD-raw − CENTROID-raw:** distribution vs mean, both unscaled.
  This is the claim D035 was meant to test.
- **P2 CLOUD-raw − CLOUD:** per-pair scaling inside the cloud, with the
  distribution held fixed. It tests whether the method's scale-free
  statistic helps or hurts selection.
- **CLOUD − CENTROID-raw** is D035's P1, restated (no new verdict). Together
  the three close the triangle: P1 − P2 = D035's P1 exactly, on the same
  counts and shared draws (an arithmetic check, not a new estimate).

## What counts as an answer

### Closed-set (primary)

Same endpoint, draws and readouts as D035, for direct comparability:
- **quantity:** mean over `k` = 1..8 of Δ mean top-1 (85-way), attention;
- **bootstrap:** D023 hierarchical config × seed with D031's v2 draws,

| outcome (each of P1, P2) | criterion |
|---|---|
| **BETTER** | attention lower bound > 0 (mean over `k` = 1..8) **and** linear mean-`k` point > 0 |
| **PARITY** | attention interval ⊂ [−δ, +δ] (mean over the differing `k`) |
| **WORSE** | attention upper bound < 0 **and** linear mean-`k` point < 0 |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | otherwise; if attention alone resolves, R reports it as "attention BETTER/WORSE, linear disagrees" |

**Likely causes (hypotheses, not tested here).**
1. **Why the centroid beats the raw cloud for trained readouts.**
   - Energy distance responds to every distributional difference, not only location. Two models with the same
     mean but different spread across configs (how strongly a model reacts to persona prompts, temperature,
     RAG) score as separated.
   - That spread pattern is tied to the specific S_build configs. S_test configs are disjoint (I2), so
     spread-based separation transfers less well than the mean shift. The mean is the config-averaged,
     config-invariant part of a model's behaviour.
   - Trained classifiers see all 75 configs anyway and learn the spread themselves. What selection most needs to
     supply is queries whose models differ *consistently*, and the centroid measures that directly.
   - Testable without generation: per query, split the raw energy into the mean-shift part (‖Δμ‖) and the
     remainder, and check whether the chains differ by that share.
2. **Why the scaling matters only open-set.**
   - The scale-free statistic is a signal-to-noise ratio: between-model distance over within-model spread.
   - A trained readout learns its own per-query weighting, so the selection's scaling is largely undone in
     training (closed-set PARITY).
   - Readout ② cannot reweight. It sums raw point-to-cloud energies over the chain, so a high-spread query adds
     a large, noisy term to every score. Raw selection favours large-distance queries, which tend to be
     high-spread ones; scale-free selection avoids them.

     finding that scaling *inside the readout* hurts (② sf 15–19 pp below ② raw on S_val): there, the division
     is by the candidate's spread, which favours high-spread candidates. Here it is at selection, pair-symmetric.
3. **Why D024 (v1) differed.**

   - On v2, with 48 more models, many of them near-relatives, the hard pairs are more often separated by
