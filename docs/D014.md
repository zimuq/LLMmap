## D — The question

> **Is the absence of any CVaR benefit at γ=0.5 (D013, `GreedyCover`) a
> property of γ — a threshold or plateau in how γ shapes selection — or
> an accident of that one nested greedy chain? Answer it for
> `GreedyCover` and for `JointGreedy`.**

### What is and is not known

- **Known.** On one chain, `GreedyCover` at γ=0.5 gave no benefit over
  γ=1.0 (if anything slightly worse); γ ∈ {0.25, 0.1, 0.05} all gave the
  full benefit; γ=1.0 is decisively worse.
- **Not known.** Anything about γ between 0.25 and 1.0 except that one
  point. D008's proxy anomaly and D013's trained anomaly are **the same
  chain evaluated two ways** — not an independent replication of "γ=0.5
  is bad." Chains are composition-unstable (D008/R5: half the selected
  queries change when half the build configs do), and one weak early
  pick cascades through a nested greedy chain. TACC's mechanism (CVaR₀.₅
  "commits to neither mean nor tail") predicts a result *between* γ=1
  and γ=0.25, not at or below γ=1 — so the observation is not yet
  explained.

### Why both algorithms — and why that is the sharpest test available

`JointGreedy` has never been run at γ=0.5. More importantly, the two
algorithms select **different** chains for the same γ from `k=2` on
(they coincide only at `k=1`, D010/R2). "Chain luck" is therefore
independent across algorithms. **If the same γ-region fails for both,
luck is a poor explanation; if only one fails, it is chain-specific.**
Cross-algorithm agreement is an independent test of the chain-luck
hypothesis that no amount of extra γ points on a single algorithm can
give.

### Pre-registered hypotheses (stated before any new run)

- **H1 — threshold / plateau.** Benefit versus γ is a step. Above some
  cutoff γ\* (somewhere in 0.25–0.5) selection behaves like γ=1 (no
  benefit); below it, the full benefit. *Predicts:* grid points on
  **both** sides of 0.5 close to it (0.6, 0.75 above; 0.45, 0.4 below
  down to γ\*) also show no benefit; a single transition; resampled
  chains at γ=0.5 are consistently poor; the effect appears in both
  algorithms (γ\* may differ).
- **H2 — chain luck.** γ=0.5 is an isolated dip on a smoother curve.
  *Predicts:* neighbours on one or both sides recover benefit in line
  with the trend; resampled chains at γ=0.5 span a wide range that
  overlaps γ=0.25's; the other algorithm is not poor at 0.5.
- **Design-side lean, weakly held (~60/40 for H2):** chains are
  composition-unstable, TACC's mechanism does not predict the observed
  sign, and nothing else supports a discontinuity. Recorded so a wrong
  guess is visible — D012 and D013's pre-registered predictions were
  each at least partly wrong.

## What counts as an answer

Primary metric: **mean top-1** (worst-class is too noisy to decide on —
±0.20 CIs, A6 — reported, not used). Reuse D012's machinery: `δ = 0.02`
TOST equivalence, the "CI excludes 0 **and** exceeds the seed range"
rule for a resolved difference, and F4's consistent-sign-across-`k` rule
for any ordering claim. **The exact numeric labelling rule is fixed in
P1 (Call 1), before any training run, and does not move afterwards.**

Each grid γ is labelled, per algorithm, against γ=1.0: **BENEFIT**,
**NO-BENEFIT**, or **UNRESOLVED**. Then, over the ordered grid:

| Outcome (per algorithm) | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **STEP (H1)** | NO-BENEFIT for a contiguous run from γ=1.0 downward, then BENEFIT below a single cutoff, at most one grid point between; resampled-chain distribution at the plateau γ's sits clearly below the low-γ's. |
| **DIP (H2)** | A BENEFIT label at some γ *above* a NO-BENEFIT label (e.g. 0.5 sits between two BENEFIT neighbours), **or** the resampled chains at γ=0.5 substantially overlap γ=0.25's. |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | Too many UNRESOLVED points to tell STEP from DIP. Report which points, not a forced call. |

**Cross-algorithm reading, stated in advance:** STEP in both → γ is a
real dial with a cutoff (a mechanism clue). DIP in both, or STEP in one
and DIP in the other → the γ=0.5 result was chain-specific, and
"γ ≤ 0.25" remains a conservative rule with no measured cutoff behind it.

## Required steps

**S1 — Tensor-level chain analysis (free; report before any training).**
Select chains on the full frozen tensor at a fine γ grid for both
algorithms (proposed: 0.3, 0.35, 0.4, 0.45, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75 in addition
to the existing points; TACC may adjust in Call 1, but the grid **must
include points on both sides of 0.5 at close spacing** and at least one
point ≥ 0.75). Report per chain: `k=4` and `k=8` set overlap with the
γ=1.0 chain and with the γ=0.25 chain; the per-`k` objective trace
(D012/F1 found `JointGreedy`'s objective peak moves with γ: 1/3/8/8 —
report whether it does here); and which γ's yield **identical chains**
(train once, report per γ, as D012/F2 did). This alone may show whether
selection "flips" between γ=1-like and γ=0.25-like chains at some γ.

**S2 — Arm A: fine-grid trained sweep.** Train every distinct new chain
via D012/D013's exact protocol (`hparams` hash asserted equal to D009's,
same split, same 5 seeds, `k=1..8`). Reuse existing points: `GreedyCover`
γ ∈ {1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05}; `JointGreedy` γ ∈ {1.0, 0.25, 0.1,
0.05}. (`JointGreedy` γ=0.5 is new.)

**S3 — Arm B: chain stability under resampling.** For each algorithm and
γ ∈ {1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.35, 0.25, 0.1} (TACC may adjust), draw `R` random
half-subsamples of the **build** configs **without replacement** (with-
replacement resampling duplicates points and biases energy distance),
re-select the chain using *only* that subsample (I2: val/test never
touched by selection), and evaluate each chain at `k=4` and `k=8` with
D008's 1-NN proxy against the full build reference. Report per γ the
distribution (median, IQR, range) of chain quality. **Proxy is for
within-algorithm ranking across γ only** — D009/R2 showed the
proxy→trained shift is condition-dependent, so never compare proxy
levels across algorithms. **Call 2:** propose `R`, and which resampled
chains to train to confirm the proxy ordering.

**S4 — Apply the pre-registered rule** (Call 1) per algorithm; state the
cross-algorithm reading.

**S5 — Write `## R`:** per-algorithm verdict, both hypotheses' dispositions
stated plainly, S1's overlap/identity findings, Arm B's distributions,
and a plain statement of what this implies for the held `METHOD.md §5.2`
sentence — **stated, not edited.**
