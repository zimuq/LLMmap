## D — The question

> **Is the absence of any CVaR benefit at γ=0.5 (D013, `GreedyCover`) a
> property of γ — a threshold or plateau in how γ shapes selection — or
> an accident of that one nested greedy chain? Answer it for
> `GreedyCover` and for `JointGreedy`.**

### What is and is not known

- **Known.** On one chain, `GreedyCover` at γ=0.5 gave no benefit over
  γ=1.0 (if anything slightly worse); γ ∈ {0.25, 0.1, 0.05} all gave the
  full benefit; γ=1.0 is decisively worse.
- **Not known.** Anything about γ between 0.25 and 1.0 except that one
  point. D008's proxy anomaly and D013's trained anomaly are **the same
  chain evaluated two ways** — not an independent replication of "γ=0.5
  is bad." Chains are composition-unstable (D008/R5: half the selected
  queries change when half the build configs do), and one weak early
  pick cascades through a nested greedy chain. TACC's mechanism (CVaR₀.₅
  "commits to neither mean nor tail") predicts a result *between* γ=1
  and γ=0.25, not at or below γ=1 — so the observation is not yet
  explained.

### Why both algorithms — and why that is the sharpest test available

`JointGreedy` has never been run at γ=0.5. More importantly, the two
algorithms select **different** chains for the same γ from `k=2` on
(they coincide only at `k=1`, D010/R2). "Chain luck" is therefore
independent across algorithms. **If the same γ-region fails for both,
luck is a poor explanation; if only one fails, it is chain-specific.**
Cross-algorithm agreement is an independent test of the chain-luck
hypothesis that no amount of extra γ points on a single algorithm can
give.

**Correction, 2026-09-19 (P1 F1) — this overstated the independence.**
Both algorithms are greedy walks over the *same* frozen tensor built from
the *same* corpus and config sample, so noise in that tensor is shared:
a query that is spuriously strong under a given γ tends to be picked by
both (measured `k`=8 overlap is 2–4 of 8). And at `k`=1 the two are the
same algorithm (D010/R2), so any shared first-query behaviour is one
observation, not two. Cross-algorithm agreement (read at `k` ≥ 2 only)
is therefore **evidence against chain luck, not a refutation of it.**
The stronger test is Arm B, which perturbs the shared data itself.

### Pre-registered hypotheses (stated before any new run)

- **H1 — threshold / plateau.** Benefit versus γ is a step. Above some
  cutoff γ\* (somewhere in 0.25–0.5) selection behaves like γ=1 (no
  benefit); below it, the full benefit. *Predicts:* grid points on
  **both** sides of 0.5 close to it (0.6, 0.75 above; 0.45, 0.4 below
  down to γ\*) also show no benefit; a single transition; resampled
  chains at γ=0.5 are consistently poor; the effect appears in both
  algorithms (γ\* may differ).
- **H2 — chain luck.** γ=0.5 is an isolated dip on a smoother curve.
  *Predicts:* neighbours on one or both sides recover benefit in line
  with the trend; resampled chains at γ=0.5 span a wide range that
  overlaps γ=0.25's; the other algorithm is not poor at 0.5.
- **Design-side lean, weakly held (~60/40 for H2):** chains are
  composition-unstable, TACC's mechanism does not predict the observed
  sign, and nothing else supports a discontinuity. Recorded so a wrong
  guess is visible — D012 and D013's pre-registered predictions were
  each at least partly wrong.

## What counts as an answer

Primary metric: **mean top-1** (worst-class is too noisy to decide on —
±0.20 CIs, A6 — reported, not used). Reuse D012's machinery: `δ = 0.02`
TOST equivalence, the "CI excludes 0 **and** exceeds the seed range"
rule for a resolved difference, and F4's consistent-sign-across-`k` rule
for any ordering claim. **The exact numeric labelling rule is fixed in
P1 (Call 1), before any training run, and does not move afterwards.**

Each grid γ is labelled, per algorithm, against γ=1.0: **BENEFIT**,
**NO-BENEFIT**, or **UNRESOLVED**. Then, over the ordered grid:

| Outcome (per algorithm) | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **STEP (H1)** | NO-BENEFIT for a contiguous run from γ=1.0 downward, then BENEFIT below a single cutoff, at most one grid point between; resampled-chain distribution at the plateau γ's sits clearly below the low-γ's. |
| **DIP (H2)** | A BENEFIT label at some γ *above* a NO-BENEFIT label (e.g. 0.5 sits between two BENEFIT neighbours), **or** the resampled chains at γ=0.5 substantially overlap γ=0.25's. |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | Too many UNRESOLVED points to tell STEP from DIP. Report which points, not a forced call. |

**Cross-algorithm reading, stated in advance:** STEP in both → γ is a
real dial with a cutoff (a mechanism clue). DIP in both, or STEP in one
and DIP in the other → the γ=0.5 result was chain-specific, and
"γ ≤ 0.25" remains a conservative rule with no measured cutoff behind it.

**Amendment, 2026-09-19 (Review below) — Arm A alone cannot decide
H1 vs H2; Arm B decides.** P1's S1 showed chains fall into *families*
that recur across neighbouring γ (GreedyCover: {0.75, 0.6, 0.5} share a
first query; γ=0.6 and γ=0.5 give the identical `k`=8 set). So "the
neighbours on both sides of 0.5" are largely the *same chain*, not new
independent draws: a bad family would show up as NO-BENEFIT across all of
it and BENEFIT below it — a STEP over γ that is equally what chain luck
produces. The design's earlier prediction ("neighbours recover under H2")
is therefore near-void on the upper side. Consequently:
- **Arm A's labels are descriptive; they never decide H1 vs H2.**
- **The verdict per algorithm is set by Arm B**, under a rule fixed now,
  before any Arm B run (paired, over the 30 resamples, both `k`=4 and 8):
  `d_r = Q_r(γ=0.25) − Q_r(γ=0.5)` with a 95 % bootstrap CI of the mean.
  **SYSTEMATIC (→ H1)** = CI excludes 0 with mean `d` > 0.02 at both `k`;
  **CHAIN-SPECIFIC (→ H2)** = CI within ±0.02 at both `k`; else
  **UNRESOLVED**. **Positive control:** the paired contrast
  `Q_r(0.25) − Q_r(1.0)` must be SYSTEMATIC at both `k`; if Arm B cannot
  reproduce the known γ=1.0-vs-γ=0.25 gap it has no power and is reported
  as uninformative. Also report the frozen full-data γ=0.5 chain's
  percentile within the resampled γ=0.5 distribution (descriptive).
- **Combination.** H1 needs Arm B SYSTEMATIC; H2 needs Arm B
  CHAIN-SPECIFIC; Arm B UNRESOLVED or failing its control ⇒
  INCONCLUSIVE for that algorithm, **whatever Arm A shows.** A monotone
  Arm A with ≥2 UNRESOLVED points between plateau and benefit region,
  plus Arm B SYSTEMATIC, is reported as **RAMP** (a graded dial), not
  INCONCLUSIVE — the earlier table had no cell for it.
- **Either way the held `METHOD.md §5.2` sentence must change** ("a clean
  continuous ablation axis" is refuted by STEP, by DIP, and by
  chain-level noise alike); what differs is the correct replacement.

### Technical log

**Why chain families defeat Arm A.** S1: first query is 123 at γ=1.0, 117
for {0.75, 0.6, 0.5}, 114 for {0.45…0.25}, 193 for {0.1, 0.05}; GreedyCover
γ=0.6 and 0.5 select the identical `k`=8 set, as do {0.45, 0.4, 0.35}.
Under chain luck, an unlucky family {0.75, 0.6, 0.5} yields NO-BENEFIT on
all three and BENEFIT below — a STEP. Under a systematic γ effect the same
labels appear. The pattern is identical, so it cannot decide. Only
perturbing the data the chains are built from (Arm B) separates them.
S1 is otherwise neutral on H1/H2; the ~60/40 lean toward H2 is unchanged.

**Why Arm B is also the better answer to the shared-noise problem in F1.**
The two algorithms share the tensor, so their agreement is confounded by
shared estimation noise; resampling the build configs perturbs exactly
that shared noise. A paired contrast within each resample removes
resample-level difficulty, which a comparison of marginal distributions
would not.

**The numeric thresholds** reuse the project's own bound (`δ` = 0.02,
"the smallest difference this project has resolved") rather than
inventing one; the positive control (γ=1.0 vs 0.25) guards against an
underpowered Arm B being read as "chain-specific" when it is merely blind.

**F2's discriminator** (boundary at 0.5/0.45 ⇒ early picks; at 0.4/0.35 ⇒
objective shape) is a sound pre-registration but it does *not* separate H1
from H2 — discrete chain families appear under both. It also applies
differently by algorithm: `GreedyCover`'s objective is monotone at every
γ, so "objective shape" is unavailable to it; only `JointGreedy`'s
objective-peak transition (γ ≥ 0.4 peaks at `k`=1) can be tested against
it. A boundary for `GreedyCover` at 0.4/0.35 would be unexplained by
either reading and should be reported as such.

**Cost.** ≈41 min Arm A + a few minutes Arm B + ~2 min for the sanity
check; guardrail 3 GPU-hours. Running the identical-set duplicates is
right: γ=0.6 at `k`=8 is a free replication of D013's γ=0.5 (same chain —
a replication of the measurement, not independent evidence about γ).
