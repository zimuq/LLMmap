## D — The question

> **Using S_build embeddings only, does a per-query, per-pair additive
> evidence score — summed over a chain's queries — rank the real trained
> per-pair accuracy (D016: 666 pairs, `k`=8, three chains) at least as
> well as the two statistics this project's selection algorithms
> actually optimise: MAX coverage over `S_energy` (GreedyCover) and the
> joint scale-free energy over the set (JointGreedy)?**

### Why this is worth doing, precisely

The model being tested: if, given the model `v`, responses to different
queries carry roughly independent evidence, then the pairwise Bayes error
decays like `exp(−Σ_q C_q(v,v'))`, where `C_q` depends only on query `q`
and the pair. That gives three things the current statistics cannot:

- **Evidence accumulates.** MAX assumes one query suffices per pair. That
  is the likeliest reason GreedyCover is the weak link, which D017/D020 have
  now shown three times. Under additivity it is simply the wrong aggregator.
- **Linear in the set indicator.** "Maximise the worst-γ pairs' total
  evidence subject to `Σ x_q = k`" is an ILP (259 binaries, 666
  constraints, CVaR is LP-representable). It can be solved exactly, and its
  LP relaxation bounds how far any greedy chain is from optimal. JointGreedy's
  statistic has a square root inside a mean, so it is not linear in the set.
- **Probability units.** Under the Gaussian model below, the predicted
  pairwise error is `Φ(−√A/2)`. That gives "queries needed to reach X%"
  before any training run.

Additivity can fail for known reasons. Responses in one config share a
system prompt, so they are not conditionally independent given `v`.
Separately, Mahalanobis estimates from 75 points per model in high
dimension are biased. **This D is the gate that checks both before
anything is built on the framework.**

It also gives a cheap, partial handle on the "interaction" question D010
left open. Comparing the block-diagonal (additive) score with a
full-covariance joint score on the same features measures how much
cross-query dependence changes the evidence. The 09-22 review's caveat
applies: dependence is not the same thing as *useful* interaction, so
report this descriptively only.

### The estimator — fixed here, not left to P

For each query `q`, on **S_build only** (37 models × 75 configs, tok200
response embeddings, unnormalised, exactly the arrays D007 built
`S_energy_tok200` from):

1. **PCA per query**, fit on all 2,775 S_build points of that query.
   Primary `d = 64`.
2. **Pooled within-model covariance** `Σ_q` in that space: class means
   removed, pooled over all 37 models, Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage.
3. **Cross-fitted Mahalanobis²**, which is unbiased where a plug-in
   estimate is not. Plug-in `Δμᵀ Σ⁻¹ Δμ` from 75 points is inflated by
   roughly `d·(1/n_v + 1/n_v') ≈ 1.7` per query at `d=64`, about 14 over
   8 queries, which would saturate `Φ`. Instead, using D007's own config
   halves (`split_a`/`split_b`, seed 20260907):
   `M²_q(v,v') = (μ_v^A − μ_v'^A)ᵀ Σ_q⁻¹ (μ_v^B − μ_v'^B)`.
   Take `Σ_q` from the full S_build, or cross-fitted as well if P shows
   that matters (Call, see Notes). Values can be negative for
   near-identical pairs. Keep the sign.
4. **Additive evidence of a set** `Q`: `A_Q(v,v') = Σ_{q∈Q} M²_q(v,v')`.
   Predicted pairwise error: `Φ(−√max(A,0) / 2)`.
5. **Joint-Gaussian comparison, a dependence check and not a candidate
   method:** concatenate the chain's per-query 64-d PCA features (512-d at
   `k`=8). Fit one full Ledoit–Wolf covariance and compute the same
   cross-fitted `J_Q(v,v')`. At `k`=1, `J ≡ A` exactly; **assert it.**

Predictors compared, each evaluated on the **same chain's own query set**:

| name | definition | source |
|---|---|---|
| `MAX` | `max_{q∈Q} S_energy_sf[q][p]` | frozen D007 tensor, what GreedyCover optimises |
| `JOINT-E` | joint scale-free energy over `Q` | `LLMmap/joint_statistic.py` on S_build, what JointGreedy optimises |
| `ADD` | `A_Q(p)` | **primary, new** |
| `JOINT-G` | `J_Q(p)` | dependence check, new |

### What is and is not known going in

- D016 already measured tensor-proxy vs trained correlation (Spearman
  +0.75–0.85 inside the structural set, +0.55–0.70 outside) for the
  statistic it used. This D recomputes `MAX` and `JOINT-E` on exactly the
  primary analysis's footing, so all four predictors are compared the same
  way. **Do not import D016's numbers as the incumbents' values.**
- **Targets are exploratory, not confirmatory.** D016's trained
  accuracies come from the 25-config S_test that D009–D020 have inspected
  repeatedly (09-22 review; design-side review M5). This D may decide
  direction. Its numbers must not be quoted as a confirmatory result; that
  version runs on the fresh S_test (future D) once chains are frozen.
- **Ceiling.** Most of the 666 pairs sit at or near 1.0 trained accuracy,
  so Spearman is carried by the non-ceiling minority. S0 reports the tie
  count.

## What counts as an answer

**Primary analysis.** For each chain `c ∈ {paper8, coverage (GreedyCover
γ=0.1), joint_energy (JointGreedy γ=0.1)}` at `k`=8 and each of the 666
pairs, the target `y` is D016's 5-seed mean two-logit trained accuracy.
Compute Spearman ρ(predictor, y) per chain (average ranks for ties). The
pooled statistic is the **mean of the three per-chain ρ**, not one
correlation over stacked cells, because chains differ in accuracy level.

**Uncertainty must respect dyadic dependence.** Pairs that share a model
are not independent, so an i.i.d. pair bootstrap is not acceptable. Use
either a leave-one-model-out jackknife (37 folds) or a model-level
bootstrap. **P picks one and fixes it before `ADD` is ever correlated with
the target.**

**Pre-registered outcomes** (Δρ = pooled ρ difference, 95% interval, δ =
0.05):

| outcome | criterion | reading |
|---|---|---|
| **GO** | Δρ(`ADD` − `JOINT-E`) lower bound > −δ **and** Δρ(`ADD` − `MAX`) lower bound > 0 | Additive evidence is non-inferior to the best current set statistic and beats per-query MAX. The framework is worth building on |
| **NO-GO** | Δρ(`ADD` − `JOINT-E`) upper bound < −δ | Additivity loses real signal that the joint statistic keeps. Do not build ILP or adaptive work on it |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | anything else | Report per chain; do not force a call |

Report the per-chain point estimates alongside the pooled result. A GO
whose sign is inconsistent across the three chains must say so in its
headline.

**Why δ = 0.05 on ρ, not the project's usual 0.02.** 0.02 is this
project's TOST margin on *accuracy* (`DECISIONS.md` convention). It is not
calibrated for a rank correlation over ~666 heavily tied pairs. The guard
against picking an unsatisfiable margin (D008's lesson) is in S0: measure
the interval half-width of the **incumbent** ρ(`JOINT-E`) alone, which
involves no `ADD` value, and **stop for a Call if it exceeds δ.**

**Secondary (descriptive, not gating):**

- (a) **Dependence.** The distribution of `J/A` over the 666 pairs per
  chain at `k`=8, and ρ(`JOINT-G`) vs ρ(`ADD`).
- (b) **Non-ceiling subset.** Pairs with min over the three chains of
  `y` < 0.99, fixed now. Same four Spearmans.
- (c) **Calibration.** Binned `Φ(−√A/2)` vs `1 − y`. Is it
  systematically optimistic, as dependence would predict?
- (d) **k-curve.** Per chain, `k`=1..8: predicted mean 37-way accuracy
  under the fitted block-diagonal Gaussian model (closed-form or Monte
  Carlo from S_build statistics only) against the trained mean top-1
  already stored (attention: D009/D010/D018; linear: D017/D020). Report
  rank agreement over (chain, k) and the absolute gap. No retraining.
- (e) **Sensitivity.** `d ∈ {32, 128}`, and the per-pair-pooled
  covariance variant. Report these; **never choose among them by fit to
  the target.**

## Constraints

**Applicable invariants.**

- **I2:** every estimator uses S_build only. No S_val or S_test embeddings
  are read. Targets are already-stored S_test-derived results; no new
  evaluation on S_test.
- **I3:** point-cloud based. Class means and within-model covariance come
  from 75 configs per model; cross-fitting uses the cloud's two halves.
  Nothing collapses to single-response comparisons.
- **I4:** untouched. `ADD` is a separate statistic answering a different
  question, per the D010/JointGreedy precedent. GreedyCover is not modified.
- **I5/I6:** frozen embeddings, frozen corpus, chains reused verbatim.

**Out of scope, even though cheap:**
- **Solving the ILP or selecting any new chain.** A new chain needs a
  trained evaluation, which is a separate D, conditional on GO.
- Any training, any generation, any new embedding, the detectability
  check (a separate Tier-1 item), and the fresh S_test.

**Cost:** CPU only. PCA and 64×64 covariances for 259 queries, 666-pair
contractions, one 512-d joint fit per chain × `k`, and a modest Monte
Carlo for (d). Expected well under a node-hour. **State the measured
cost in `R`.**

## Notes for whoever writes P

- **Forking paths are the main risk.** `d`, shrinkage, the covariance
  pooling, the cross-fit split and the uncertainty method are fixed above
  or fixed in P before the target is touched. Nothing is tuned to
  maximise correlation. If P thinks a fixed choice is wrong, raise it as a
  Call, not a silent change.
- **Call welcome on `Σ_q` cross-fitting.** Estimating `Σ_q` on the full
  S_build while the means are cross-fitted leaves a small dependence
  between the numerator and `Σ⁻¹`. If P can show the difference is
  negligible, as a pilot on a handful of queries, say so. Otherwise propose
  fitting `Σ_q` per half as well.
- **Use the scale-free energy everywhere** (D007/F3 note in
  `tensor_tok200.meta.json`). For `JOINT-E`, use the accumulator path in
  `LLMmap/joint_statistic.py` that D010's selection used, on S_build.
- **Signed values.** Sums use signed `M²`. Floor at 0 only inside `Φ`.
- **The label discipline from D018 applies.** The script's stored verdict
  strings must implement the full pre-registered rule, or `R` must say
  they do not.
- **P must be executable by a fresh session with no conversation
  context**, per `CLAUDE.md`.
