## D — The question

> **Using a classifier-free, *set-level* (multivariate) two-sample
> statistic over the same frozen I5 embeddings — not `GreedyCover`'s
> current MAX-aggregation of independent per-query separability scores
> (`METHOD.md §5.1`, invariant I4) — can a greedy search select a
> `k`-query set whose downstream closed-set accuracy, evaluated through
> D009's exact trained-pipeline protocol, narrows or closes the gap
> against the paper's own 8 queries, while remaining cheap (no
> per-candidate classifier retraining)?**

This tests the leading explanation for D009's tie: `Sep(q,v,v')` is
computed **per query**, and `cov(p) = max_{q} S[q][p]` (I4) deliberately
never asks whether queries work *together* — it only asks whether any
one of them already works alone. Algorithm H.1's retrain-per-candidate
loop implicitly answers the "together" question, at 372 real training
runs. This D asks whether a classifier-free statistic can answer a
version of the same question cheaply enough to stay inside CDQD's
actual cost budget.

### Why this exists now

D009 confirmed the tensor-level proxy is a sound *ranking* instrument
(Spearman 0.95–0.98 vs. the trained result) and that CVaR-coverage
beats every classifier-free baseline (mean-greedy, random) cleanly. The
one place it didn't win outright — the paper's own 8 — is the one place
the opposing method had access to something CVaR-coverage structurally
lacks: interaction signal, bought at real training cost. Before treating
the tie as the final word (`DECISIONS.md` D5 already frames it as a
strong result on its own terms), this D checks whether that gap has a
cheap fix or not, without committing to the expensive route (Direction
B — an actual classifier-in-the-loop search on our own pool — which the
human has explicitly shelved, not folded into this D).

### What's already decided and does not block this D

- **A4** (query efficiency primary): unaffected — this D reports
  whatever the four `METHOD.md §8` metrics show, same as D008/D009.
- **Training pipeline and protocol**: fixed, reuse D009's exactly —
  same `hparams` hash, same `S_build`/`S_val`/`S_test` split (I2), same
  `LLMmap/trainer.py`/`inference_model_archs.py`, same 5-seed scheme
  (Call 2 precedent). **No new baseline runs** — `random`, `mean-greedy`,
  `paper8`, and `CVaR`'s numbers are D009's own
  `results/D009/metrics_by_condition_k.json`, reused verbatim (I6). This
  D produces exactly one new condition's numbers and compares them in.
- **This does not modify `GreedyCover` or I4's existing MAX-aggregated
  coverage machinery.** The new statistic is a second, parallel
  selection criterion evaluated *alongside* CVaR-coverage, not a
  replacement for it — see Constraints for why this is flagged rather
  than treated as a routine implementation detail.

## What counts as an answer

| Outcome | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **CONFIRMED** | The joint-statistic-selected `k`-query set, evaluated through D009's trained pipeline, materially closes or reverses the gap against the paper's 8 (currently +0.007 mean top-1 at `k=8`, not resolved against seed range) at one or more `k`, at a cost that stays classifier-free during selection (only the final evaluation trains, same as every other D009 condition). The interaction-blindness hypothesis is right and fixable inside CDQD's existing cost budget. |
| **FALSIFIED** | The joint-statistic condition performs about the same as, or worse than, plain CVaR-coverage. **This is informative, not a failure** — it would mean query-interaction signal, if it's real, isn't recoverable from a classifier-free two-sample statistic on concatenated embeddings, and closing the paper-8 gap (if that's ever a goal) needs actual classifier-in-the-loop signal — i.e. Direction B, still a human call, not triggered automatically by this result. |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | The joint statistic is numerically degenerate before a clean comparison is possible — e.g. a curse-of-dimensionality collapse as `k` grows (see S1's mandatory pilot) that makes the statistic uninformative rather than merely weaker. Distinguish explicitly from FALSIFIED, which requires the statistic to be verified non-degenerate first. |

## Required steps

**S1 — Mandatory pilot: check the joint statistic isn't degenerate
before running anything at scale.** Concatenating `k` queries' 1024-d
response embeddings gives a `k×1024`-d joint vector per config (8192-d
at `k=8`), computed from only ~75 build configs per model. High
dimension, small sample size is a known failure mode for distance-based
statistics (pairwise distances concentrate, losing discriminative
power). On a small subset of pairs and `k` values, verify the joint
energy-distance statistic still shows a real separation-vs-noise gap
(e.g. against a shuffled/null baseline) as `k` grows. **If it degrades
sharply, say so and stop before the full run — this is what would
produce INCONCLUSIVE, not a green light to push through anyway.**

**S2 — Implement the joint statistic.** For a candidate set `Q_k` and
pair `(v, v')`: form point clouds `P(Q_k, v) = { concat(E(o_{q1,s}), …,
E(o_{qk,s})) : s ∈ S_build }` (one joint vector per config, not per
query) and compute a two-sample statistic between `P(Q_k,v)` and
`P(Q_k,v')`. **Call 1 (flagged for design-side review):** which
statistic — energy distance (this project's established default,
`PAPER_DEVIATIONS.md` item 10, recommended for comparability) vs. an
alternative (e.g. MMD, D008's own robustness-check statistic). State
the choice and why, don't default silently.

**S3 — Build the greedy search around it.** At each step, evaluate
every remaining candidate query's *marginal* effect on the joint
statistic across all pairs (CVaR-weighted, same `γ` as the project
default) when added to the current set, and pick the best. **Call 2
(flagged):** the naive version recomputes the joint statistic for every
candidate at every step — measure this cost in S1's pilot before
committing to the full `k=1..8` grid; propose a cheaper approximation
if the naive version is too slow, with a stated accuracy/cost
trade-off, not silently.

**S4 — Produce the nested `k=1..8` chain** from S3, on the frozen
corpus (I6 — no new generation, no re-embedding).

**S5 — Evaluate via D009's exact trained-pipeline protocol.** Same
`hparams`, same split, same 5-seed scheme, same `hparams` hash check
D009's R3 used to confirm identical training procedure across
conditions. This produces the one new condition's numbers.

**S6 — Compare directly against D009's existing four conditions**
(reused verbatim, not rerun) — CVaR-coverage and the paper's 8
specifically, at every `k` computed. State the outcome per the table
above.

**S7 — Write `## R`:** verdict, S1's degeneracy check, Call 1/2
dispositions, the new condition's full metrics, and the direct
comparison against D009's stored numbers.

## Constraints

**Applicable invariants:** **I4 — flagged explicitly, not a violation.**
I4 governs how a query *set's* coverage of a pair is aggregated from
*independent per-query* scores (MAX, never sum/mean) — its own
rationale (`METHOD.md §5.1`) is that a query uniquely good for one hard
pair shouldn't be diluted by averaging against weaker queries on that
same pair. The statistic this D introduces answers a **different**
question — whether queries combined *reveal* separation none of them
shows alone — and does not aggregate per-query scores at all, so it
does not contradict I4's rule. It is nonetheless close enough to I4's
subject matter (what "a query set's separability" means) that this D
is drafted as `gate: REVIEW` and flagged here for the record, per
`CLAUDE.md`'s standing practice around anything invariant-adjacent —
proceeding per the human's explicit 2026-09-10 direction to draft this,
not withheld pending further sign-off.

I2 (same split as every prior measurement), I5 (same frozen I5
embeddings, no substitution), I6 (D009's baseline conditions reused
verbatim — do not re-run them; only the new condition executes).

**Out of scope:** Direction B — an actual classifier-in-the-loop
search (Algorithm-H.1-style retraining per candidate) on our own pool —
is explicitly shelved by human decision, 2026-09-10. Do not run it
inside this D even if S1's pilot suggests the joint statistic is weak;
that would be a separate, new D if and when the human decides to
revisit it.

**Framing requirement:** this D's comparisons against "the paper's 8"
inherit `DECISIONS.md` D5's framing — the cost-asymmetry caveat
(Algorithm H.1's 372 training runs vs. this D's classifier-free
selection) applies here exactly as it did in D009. Do not report a
narrowed or closed gap as "beating the paper" without also stating that
selection remained classifier-free throughout.

**Cost:** classifier-free computation on already-frozen embeddings
(S1–S4) plus one new set of D009-style training runs for evaluation
(S5, at whatever `k` values S1/Call 2 support) — expected small based on
D009's own pilot (8.7s/training run), but **state a measured cost from
S1's pilot before committing to the full grid**, same standing practice
as D002/D006/D007/D009.

### Technical log

**S1's pilot is exactly the check D010 asked for, done correctly.** The
distinction the outcome table needed — *degenerate* (INCONCLUSIVE) vs.
*weaker but still working* (proceed) — is precisely what AUROC vs. the
same-model null control separates, and TACC used both a ratio (median
vs. null p95) and AUROC rather than reporting only one. AUROC ≥0.985
through `k`=16 (16384-d, only 75 configs/cloud) settles the
curse-of-dimensionality risk `## Constraints` flagged; the declining
*ratio* after `k`=2 is not evidence against this — it's the source of
F1's pre-registered prediction, correctly kept as a separate finding
rather than folded into the degeneracy question.

**The additive-accumulator trick is real, not a shortcut.** Squared
Euclidean distance in a concatenated space decomposes as `‖concat(a) -
concat(b)‖² = Σᵢ ‖aᵢ - bᵢ‖²` — exactly true, and TACC correctly
identified that energy distance's *un-squared* norm means the
accumulation must happen on the squared quantity, with the square root
taken exactly once at the end, not per query. This is why it's 519×
faster (0.051ms vs. 26.201ms per cell) and why it dissolves Call 2 —
the "naive too slow, needs an approximation" branch D010 anticipated
never had to be reached, because the exact computation was reframed
rather than approximated. Verified against the direct computation
(7.09e-06 agreement), not asserted — matching this project's standing
verify-don't-assume discipline (D001/D004's representation-bias
disclosure, D009/F5's embedding-bypass check, same posture again here).

**Call 1's MMD rejection is a sound, specific argument, not a default.**
The bandwidth argument (a global `σ²` can't hold as the concatenated
space's own scale grows with `k`, and a per-`k` bandwidth reintroduces
the cross-`k` incomparability D007/F3 already ruled out) is the correct
generalization of D007/F2's fix to this new setting — TACC applied an
existing project lesson to a new structural situation rather than
re-deriving from scratch or skipping the check.

**F1 is a genuine pre-registration, and its falsification condition is
explicit** ("if the advantage instead appears only at `k=8`, the
hypothesis is not what produced it and `R` must say so") — same
discipline as D006/R11 and D007/F2, both cited by TACC as precedent
rather than invented for this D alone.

**F2 is a real structural difference from `GreedyCover` worth recording
in `METHOD.md` eventually, not just this D's `R`.** `METHOD.md §5.3`'s
"the greedy chain is nested, so it yields the accuracy-vs-`k` curve for
free" relies on MAX-aggregated coverage's monotone-submodular structure
(I4) — a property this D's joint statistic does not share by
construction (concatenating more dimensions can dilute a distance-based
statistic even as it adds information). TACC's handling (build the
chain to `k`=8 anyway for comparability, report where the objective
actually peaked, treat a non-monotone curve as a property of the
statistic rather than a bug) is correct and requires no change — but if
D010 confirms this pattern, `METHOD.md §5.3`'s "curve for free" framing
should eventually note it's a property of MAX-aggregation specifically,
not of greedy nested search in general. Not actioned now (would be a
`METHOD.md` revision, human-escalation item per `CLAUDE.md` rule (d)) —
flagged for whenever this D closes with a result worth writing up.

**F3's conservative-null disclosure is correct and, if anything,
understates the finding** — a smaller-`n` null has more sampling
variance, inflating its own p95 and *shrinking* the apparent real-vs-null
gap, so S1's already-strong AUROC numbers are a lower bound, not an
optimistic estimate.

**Cost check:** ~10 minutes total (0.03 core-h selection + ~6 minutes of
training at D009's own measured 8.7s/run) is trivial and grounded in an
actual prior measurement, not a fresh guess — approved without

### R2 — `k=1` is an exact correctness check, and it passes

At `k=1` a "set" has no interaction, so the joint statistic **reduces
mathematically to the per-query one**. Both criteria must then select the same
query and score identically. They do: both pick **q=193**, and the measured delta
is **+0.0000** — bitwise, not approximately. The machinery is doing what it claims
at the one point where the answer is known in advance.

### R3 — Peak `k` (the review's requested headline): **8 for energy, 6 for MMD**

The energy objective rises monotonically over `k`=1..8 — CVaR 0.179 → 0.233 →
0.233 → 0.234 → 0.235 → 0.239 → 0.241 → **0.243**, peaking at `k=8`. **F2's
non-monotonicity concern did not bind for the primary statistic**, though it was
real: the MMD objective peaks at `k=6` (0.1461) and *falls* at `k=7` and `k=8`.
So the risk P1 flagged materialised for the secondary statistic and not the
primary. Recording it that way rather than claiming the flag was unnecessary.

Note the objective's steep rise from `k=1` to `k=2` (+0.054) against near-flat
increments after (+0.000 to +0.005 through `k`=6). The set-level statistic finds
most of what it can find in the **second** query — consistent with the pilot's
separation ratio peaking at `k=2`.

### R4 — F1's prediction: half right, and the failing half matters

P1 registered: *if the interaction hypothesis is right, the joint chain should beat
CVaR most at small `k` (1–3) and converge by `k=8`.*

- **The small-`k` half holds.** Against the paper's 8, the two largest gaps are at
  `k=3` (+0.047) and `k=2` (+0.033), and the selection objective gains almost all
  of its value at `k=2`.
- **The convergence half fails.** The advantage does not vanish by `k=8` — it is
  +0.031 vs paper8 and +0.024 vs CVaR there, among the largest in the table.

P1 also said: *"If the advantage instead appears only at `k=8`, the hypothesis is
not what produced it."* It does not appear only at `k=8` — it is present at every
`k` — so that disqualifier is not triggered. But the clean "interaction helps
early, dimension costs later" story the pilot suggested is **not** what the trained
numbers show. Something about the joint criterion keeps paying at `k=8`, and this D
does not establish what.

### R5 — The MMD check (review amendment) agrees

Chain: `[193, 123, 233, 0, 114, 235, 140, 234]`, **5/8 overlap with the energy
chain**, and at `k=8` it scores 0.8662 — +0.0143 [−0.0004, +0.0277] against
CVaR-coverage and above the paper's 8. **The result is not an artifact of energy
distance specifically.** Energy is the stronger of the two (+0.0095), consistent
with Call 1's reasoning that MMD's bandwidth cannot be held fixed as the
concatenated space's scale grows — here it was held fixed only *within* each
greedy step, which is sufficient for the search but is a weaker instrument.

### R6 — What the joint criterion actually selected

`[193, 140, 237, 114, 233, 117, 0, 16]` — **2/8 overlap with D008's CVaR chain**.
So a substantially different set, not a reshuffle. Notably it selects **q=0, one of
the paper's own 8** (the injection-wrapped "who created you"), which the per-query
CVaR chain never picked at any `k≤8`. A query that is unremarkable alone but
complementary in a set is exactly the object I4's MAX aggregation cannot see, and
the joint criterion picked it up.
