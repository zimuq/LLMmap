> conflict with I2's discipline or T4.2's "`S_test` touched once" rule:
> that rule is about *analysis* (not looking at test results until Phase
> 4), not about *when the raw data is collected*. Decided here as a
> routine implementation-efficiency call — flag in P if this reasoning
> doesn't hold for some reason not anticipated here.

**Two findings block execution. Neither is resolvable on the TACC side.**

**F1 — `C7` is still open, and D006 would freeze it into the corpus
irreversibly.** D006 mentions C7 nowhere; its only reference to the token
budget is the cost line "100–200 tok", treated as a costing *range*. But S4
must pick one, and it is then baked into all 1,077,625 responses under I6.
`DECISIONS.md` C7 says explicitly *"Decide C7 on information grounds, not
cost — still open"*; D006 would resolve it silently at whatever `llm.py:9`
says (`max_new_tokens = 100`).

This is not hypothetical. Measured on the shipped corpus (62,394 responses):
the share of responses ending mid-sentence is **~4% at 100–299 chars** — the
stylistic baseline — and climbs monotonically to **66.5% (400–499)**, **83.4%
(500–599)**, **91.2% (600–699)**. That is right-censoring at the 100-token
cap: **~38 percentage points of the corpus is truncated.** Signal a 100-token
cap destroys: response length itself, closing/sign-off style, refusal length,
whether an enumerated answer completes.

**This project has already been bitten by exactly this.** D004's finding was
that a statistic on a censored variable measures the censoring, not the
population (M6 = 0.997 censored vs **0.616** uncensored). F1 is that same
hazard moved to the *raw material*, where no later choice of statistic can
undo it — and I7's own stated rationale is that regenerating this corpus is
"the most expensive avoidable mistake here."

**Proposal (AMENDED 2026-09-05) — C7 need not be a freeze-point decision
at all.** My first draft proposed a pilot to inform C7 before generating.
Writing up the trade-off surfaced a better answer that removes the risk
rather than measuring it.

`max_new_tokens` is a **stopping rule, not a generation parameter**: under
greedy or ancestral sampling, token *t*'s distribution depends only on
tokens `< t`, so the first 100 tokens of a 200-token sample are
distributed exactly as a 100-token sample would be. **Generate once at the
higher cap, store the full text, and C7 becomes a post-hoc analysis
parameter** — truncating stored 200-token responses to 100 reproduces the
100-token corpus exactly. The reverse is impossible.

That changes what C7 *is*: not "an irreversible choice frozen in by
I6/I7", but "the ceiling we pay to generate up to", with every value below
it recoverable free, forever. Only the ceiling is irreversible.

**Revised recommendation: generate at 200 and record C7 in the manifest as
the generation *ceiling*, leaving the analysis-time budget free below it.**
199–240 node-h (2.9–3.5% of balance), 10.7–13.0 h makespan, ~0.8 GB
instead of ~0.4 GB. I do **not** recommend a 400 ceiling — cost roughly
doubles again (~400 node-h, ~21 h, past this plan's own guardrail).

**The pilot is still worth running and now asks a sharper question:** one
pass at 400 tokens over ~6 models spanning 0.5B–14B (~1,200 generations,
~30 min + load), analysed at 100/200/400 by truncation. It reports (a)
censoring per cap — is 200 a real ceiling or just a higher wall — and (b)
**whether `Sep` under the frozen I5 embedding actually improves from
100 → 200 → 400.** (b) is the half my first draft missed, and the half
`DECISIONS.md` C7's "information grounds" actually asks for.

**Honest risks in raising the cap**, recorded so review can weigh them:
mean-pooled embeddings average over tokens, so if fingerprint signal is
concentrated in a response's *opening*, more tokens could **dilute** it and
*reduce* `Sep` — a real possibility, hence pilot question (b); e5's 512-token
training window gets crowded at 400; small models at high temperature loop,
and at 400 tokens a loop can dominate the embedding; and the cap is one more
difference from the paper's setup (already unavoidable given I5, the query
pool, and the universe all differ).

**This rests on a check, not an assumption:** greedy output at 200 truncated
to 100 must be a **character-exact prefix** of greedy output at 100. Folded
into S1's existing verification pass. If it fails, the argument is void and
C7 reverts to a genuine freeze-point decision — reported either way.

**Still a Part-C decision and still design side's to make.** What changed is
that a wrong answer is now cheap to correct in one direction only, which is
the argument for setting the ceiling at 200. **S1's pilot can run before C7
is set. S4 cannot.**

**F2 — C4 is a three-way split; the code and configs support two pools.** S3
reads as though the machinery is ready; it is not.
`confs/prompt_configurations/train_test_split.json` has exactly two keys
(`train`, `test`), `prompt_configuration.py:6` defines two constants, and
`_cond_choice` (`:256`) would `KeyError` on a third. D005 fixed a *two*-pool
split. The extension is not mechanical because the collections are thin:
`systems` 84 → 42/21/21 is fine, but `cot_prompts` is **6** (→ 2/2/2) and
`rag_prompts` is **9** (→ 3/3/3). With `COT_P = 0.2`, a 25-config `S_val`
holds ~5 CoT-bearing configs drawn from a 2-element pool — that re-opens
**exactly the A5 question** (a disjoint split of a tiny value set creating a
worse confound than the leakage it removes). **Call 3** in the full plan puts
three options up; I recommend 3-way on `systems`+`temperature` with
`cot`/`rag` split *two* ways (`build`-disjoint, `val`/`test` sharing), since
the leak I2 exists to prevent is `build` seeing the evaluation pools. **I
will implement whichever is approved and will not pick one unilaterally.**
Either way the config-schema change is an **I7 event**.

**F3 — a third silent defect in `sample()`, same class as D005's two.** Its
docstring promises "n **unique** confs" and it dedups via `set()`, but
`PromptConf` defines neither `__eq__` nor `__hash__` (verified: absent from
`__dict__`), so identity hashing means no `add` is ever rejected and the loop
deduplicates **nothing**. Measured over 40 seeds at C4's sizes: `S_build`
(n=75) carries **mean 3.95 duplicate configs** (max 8), and **33% of
duplicates land in the "bare" cell** (no system/CoT/RAG) which is only 6.2%
of draws — they concentrate where the fewest dimensions distinguish configs.
This does not invalidate D005 (its test checks drawn *indices*, unaffected),
but it means `S_build` is effectively ~71 distinct configs, baked in by I6.
**Call 4:** the dedup *key* is a judgment call — I recommend

**Needs your input — the one real decision here:** **F1 / C7 — set the
generation-length ceiling.** TACC's case: generation is causal (token *t*
depends only on tokens `<t`), so a response generated at 200 tokens and
then truncated to 100 is *identical* to one generated at 100 directly —
but not the reverse. The current 100-token default silently censors ~38
points of the corpus (measured: 66.5% of 400–499-char responses cut off
mid-sentence, rising to 91.2% by 600–699 chars), and this project has
already been burned once by exactly this shape of problem (D004: a
censored statistic measures the censoring, not the population). Baking a
low cap into the frozen corpus is a one-way door — raising it later means
regenerating everything (I7's own stated nightmare scenario). TACC
recommends generating at a **200-token ceiling** (not 400 — cost roughly
doubles again) and running a small pilot first (~6 models, ~1,200
generations, ~30 min) to check whether raising the cap actually helps or
could dilute the fingerprint signal (mean-pooled embeddings average over
tokens, so more text isn't free of risk). **My own read agrees with
TACC's — but this is a Part-C hyperparameter with real, mostly
one-directional stakes, exactly the kind of call reserved for you rather
than decided on my own.** Cost at 200: ~199–240 node-hours (~2.9–3.5% of
balance), ~10.7–13h makespan — still comfortably affordable and inside
the purge window.
